Rights and Obligations Between Husband and Wife

Title III, Family Code, Arts. 68–73 (syllabus IX A–D; 1 Tolentino 338–355). Covers the duty to live together, love, respect, fidelity, help and support (Art. 68); the family domicile (Art. 69); support of the family (Art. 70); household management (Art. 71); judicial relief against a neglectful or harmful spouse (Art. 72); and each spouse's right to a profession or business (Art. 73, as amended by RA 10572). Also covered: the Civil Code provisions these replaced (Arts. 109–117), separation in fact (Arts. 100, 127), abandonment (Arts. 101, 128), RA 9262, and how these remedies differ from legal separation.

Overview: the map of Title III and how Uribe tests it

Title III is short (six articles), but almost every word in it can be turned into a trap. Uribe tests it in three ways:

  1. Codal alterations. He changes "both" to "the husband", "court" to "family council", "community property" to "separate property", the order of the Art. 70 sources, or "valid, serious, and moral grounds" to "any ground". He also swaps in the old Civil Code rule, such as the husband fixing the residence or the parents and grandparents being consulted.
  2. Enforcement situationers. Example: "W left the conjugal abode; H sues to compel her to return. Will it prosper? What are his remedies?" The answer turns on Arroyo, Ilusorio, Goitia, Atilano and Arts. 100(1)/127(1), 101, 55(10) and 135.
  3. Cross-references. Arts. 68–71 are the "essential marital obligations" for Art. 36 (Molina). Abandonment links to Art. 55(10), Art. 101 and Art. 135(4). Support links to Arts. 94, 121, 194–198 and 204.

Tolentino (dual aspect of family relations): family relations have an internal aspect, which is natural and moral and "sacred and inaccessible even to the law" (sexual relations, mutual love, correspondence, domestic practices), and an external aspect in which third persons and the public interest are involved (support, parental authority, liability for acts of family members). The law regulates marriage only in its external aspect. This explains why most duties in Art. 68 cannot be enforced by compulsion and carry only patrimonial or indirect sanctions.

Art. 68: the personal obligations of the spouses

Art. 68, FC. The husband and wife are obliged to live together, observe mutual love, respect and fidelity, and render mutual help and support. (109a)
Quiz trap: the text says "obliged" (Tolentino's print reads "obligated"; the B29 and current codal texts read "obliged"). Uribe's one-word changes are elsewhere. Watch for "mutual respect and fidelity" with "love" dropped (that is NCC 109, not FC 68), "husband shall render support to the wife" (not mutual), and "live together at the domicile fixed by the husband" (old NCC 110).

Breakdown of Art. 68: five obligations

  1. To live together (cohabitation / consortium)
  2. Mutual love (new in the FC)
  3. Mutual respect
  4. Mutual fidelity
  5. Mutual help and support
Tolentino (source): taken from Art. 109, Civil Code, which came from Art. 56 of the Spanish Civil Code and Arts. 44–45 of the Spanish Civil Marriage Law of 1870. Under Spanish law the husband had to protect the wife and the wife had to obey the husband (Art. 48, Marriage Law of 1870). The Civil Code replaced this with "mutual respect", and the husband's duty to protect is now absorbed in mutual help. The FC added "mutual love".

1. Obligation to live together (right of cohabitation / consortium)

Marriage entitles each spouse to the other's society: a common life "under the same roof" so that both can fulfill the obligations of marriage. Consequences, per Tolentino:

  • The husband cannot throw the wife out of the conjugal abode. She cannot complain of trespass to dwelling if the husband breaks down the door to enter their own abode.
  • A husband's promise to pay his wife money if she will allow sexual intercourse or continue to cohabit with him is void for want of consideration (cause), since she would only be doing what the law already obliges her to do.
  • Scope: cohabitation includes the domestic and sexual community of the spouses, and its extent varies with circumstances. Temporary separations by necessity (a traveling salesman, a seaman, a public official, an OFW) do not breach the duty. Spouses are still "living together", even if forced apart by circumstances or money problems, if neither intends to sever marital relations permanently.
  • The duty does not restrict personal liberty or limit itself to physical union. But the law can reach only the "tangible and material aspect" of cohabitation. It "is powerless to impose that intimacy of life which is the basis of conjugal peace and happiness."

Sexual relations: the right is not absolute

Tolentino: a husband is entitled to sexual relations with his wife, but the right "is not absolute" and covers only normal intercourse. The wife may justly refuse if the husband resorts to abnormal or unnatural practices, to harmful contraceptives, or to offensive conduct. He cannot compel her when she is not in condition (e.g., ill), when excessive intercourse would endanger her health, or when he has a venereal disease. (Tolentino's older text says it is "not rape" to force the wife. That view is outdated: marital rape is punishable under the Anti-Rape Law of 1997 (RA 8353), and forced sex may also be sexual violence under RA 9262.)
Goitia v. Campos-Rueda (35 Phil. 252, 1916)

About a month after the wedding, the husband demanded that the wife perform unchaste and lascivious acts on his genital organs. When she refused anything other than normal intercourse, he maltreated her with words and blows and inflicted bodily harm. She left for her parents' home and sued for support. The trial court dismissed the case, holding that she could only have support inside the husband's home (Art. 149, old CC, gave the obligor the option of paying a pension or receiving and maintaining the recipient in his home). Doctrine: a wife who is forced to leave the conjugal abode by her husband, without fault on her part, may maintain an action against him for separate maintenance when she has no other remedy. This holds notwithstanding the obligor's option to support her in his own home. The obligation of support arises from the marriage itself and does not depend on a prior divorce or legal separation. When the husband's own conduct makes continued cohabitation intolerable, "relief in some ways should be obtainable."

Uribe (syllabus and doctrines outline): Goitia is listed twice: under legal separation ("supra") and again under IX-A, Obligation to live together. Learn it as the counter-rule to Atilano: a wife who leaves with just cause keeps her right to support and may receive it outside the conjugal home. A wife who leaves without just cause cannot claim it.
Art. 204, FC. The person obliged to give support shall have the option to fulfill the obligation either by paying the allowance fixed, or by receiving and maintaining in the family dwelling the person who has a right to receive support. The latter alternative cannot be availed of in case there is a moral or legal obstacle thereto. (299a)
Quiz trap: "The husband may always fulfill his obligation to support his wife, at his option, by receiving and maintaining her in the family dwelling." This is FALSE. The option "cannot be availed of in case there is a moral or legal obstacle thereto" (Art. 204, second sentence, which codifies Goitia). In Uribe's 2025 quiz the statement of Art. 204 without the exception was marked FALSE: "in the FC the exception is that if there is moral obstacle."

No compulsion: cohabitation cannot be enforced by force, contempt, injunction or habeas corpus

Tolentino (legal sanction for cohabitation): the only guaranty of this right is the spouses' mutual affection, so cohabitation "must be spontaneous and cannot be imposed by the law or the courts. The only possible sanction is patrimonial in nature." If the husband refuses to live with the wife, he can be made to pay her a pension and damages. If the wife refuses to live with the husband, he can refuse to support her. Where the wife abandoned the husband without justification, it is her duty to return and conform to his reasonable mode of living, not his duty to solicit her return, and until she offers to return, no obligation of support rests on him. Use of force: the Argentine Code (Art. 187) let the husband ask for police force to compel the wife to live with him. "Our code does not contain a similar provision." Without an express provision, force is not available. It would violate personal dignity and security, it would be impractical (courts cannot post a guard to prevent evasion), it could lead to maltreatment, and "coercive measures to compel the wife to live with the husband would be worse than imprisonment for debt."
Arroyo v. Vasquez de Arroyo (42 Phil. 54, August 11, 1921)

The wife left the conjugal home because of extreme jealousy but without sufficient cause. The husband sued for restitution of conjugal rights and a mandatory injunction requiring her to return. She counterclaimed for divorce (separate maintenance). The Supreme Court found no sufficient cause for her departure, so she was not entitled to separate maintenance, and it declared that she was under obligation to return to the marital domicile. Doctrine: "It is not within the province of the courts of this country to attempt to compel one of the spouses to cohabit with, and render conjugal rights to, the other." Where the property rights of one spouse are invaded, an action for restitution of such rights can be maintained. But the Court was "disinclined to sanction the doctrine that an order, enforcible by process of contempt, may be entered to compel the restitution of the purely personal right of consortium." The court can only admonish the wife (declare her duty to return) in its decision.

Ramirez-Cuaderno v. Cuaderno (12 SCRA 505, November 28, 1964)

Doctrine (Uribe's outline): "The court CANNOT impose on the husband and wife to live together EVEN if this is an obligation under the NCC (even now in FC) to live together." B29 states it as: the courts cannot compel cohabitation between the spouses, "especially if the husband maltreats the wife." Learn it as a companion of Arroyo: the duty to live together exists, but no court order can force it.

Atilano v. Chua Ching Beng (103 Phil. 255, March 29, 1958)

The wife set up her residence apart from the one her husband provided and sued for support. Doctrine: "Although the husband and the wife are obliged to live together, observe mutual respect and fidelity and render mutual help and assistance (Art. 109) and that the wife is entitled to be supported, our laws contain no provision compelling the wife to live with her husband where even without legal justification she establishes her residence apart from that provided for by the former." In that event, "there is no plausible reason why she should be allowed any support from the husband." Two lessons: (1) she cannot be compelled to return, even when she has no justification; (2) a spouse who lives apart without legal justification is not entitled to support. The second lesson is now codified in Arts. 100(1) and 127(1).

Ilusorio v. Bildner (G.R. No. 139789, May 12, 2000)

Potenciano Ilusorio, a wealthy lawyer then about 86, and his wife Erlinda had lived separately since 1972. After a five-month stay at Erlinda's house in Antipolo, Potenciano went to live in his own condominium in Makati. Erlinda petitioned for habeas corpus to have custody of him, claiming his children were keeping him from her. The CA denied the writ but granted her visitation rights enforceable by contempt. Doctrine: a writ of habeas corpus may not be used to compel a husband to live with his wife. "Marital rights including coverture and living in conjugal dwelling may not be enforced by the extra-ordinary writ of habeas corpus." Habeas corpus lies only against an illegal restraint of liberty, and a mentally competent husband who freely chooses where to live is not restrained. The Court also struck down the visitation rights, because they forced him to be visited against his will and violated his right to privacy. "No court is empowered as a judicial authority to compel a husband to live with his wife. Coverture cannot be enforced by compulsion of a writ of habeas corpus carried out by sheriffs or by any other mesne process."

Tolentino (contrast: when habeas corpus does lie): if a father keeps his daughter from her husband when she wants to live with the husband, the husband may secure her release through habeas corpus. The writ there attacks a restraint imposed by a third person against the spouse's will; it does not compel an unwilling spouse. Reconcile with Ilusorio on this basis: habeas corpus works against illegal restraint, never as a device for compelling consortium.

Tenchavez v. Escaño (15 SCRA 355, November 29, 1965)

Vicenta Escaño secretly married Pastor Tenchavez, then refused to live with him, went to the U.S., obtained a Nevada divorce while still a Filipino, and remarried there. Doctrine on Art. 68 (damages): the foreign divorce obtained by a Filipino was not recognized, so the marriage subsisted. Her refusal to perform her wifely duties, her denial of consortium and her desertion of her husband constitute in law a wrong caused through her fault, for which the husband is entitled to damages (moral damages were awarded against her; the claim against her parents was dismissed for lack of proof of malice). Lesson: the personal duties cannot be specifically enforced, but their wrongful breach may be compensated in money. Again, the sanction is patrimonial.

Tolentino (damages for refusal of cohabitation): writers are divided. Salvat holds that a wife who left without cause cannot be made to pay damages, because no law authorizes it and it is incompatible with the juridical relations of spouses. Planiol and Ripert (and Castan) hold that damages may be recovered even for mere unjustified denial of sexual relations. Philippine jurisprudence (Tenchavez) allowed damages where the desertion was wrongful.

Third persons who interfere with the marriage

Tolentino (remedies for interference): "Any person who interferes with the right of the spouses to cohabitation may be held liable for damages under article 26 of the Civil Code" (meddling with or disturbing the family relations of another; alienation of affection). Reviewer note: the elements of alienation of affection are (1) wrongful conduct of the defendant, (2) loss of affection or consortium, and (3) a causal connection. A stranger is liable regardless of good motives; a parent or close relative is liable only if malice is proven.

2–4. Mutual love, respect and fidelity

Tolentino (mutual fidelity): defined (Siete Partidas) as "the loyalty which each should observe toward the other, the wife having nothing to do with another man, nor the husband with another woman." Legal sanctions: adultery (wife) and concubinage (husband) under the RPC (Arts. 333, 334), which are not equal for the two spouses, and sexual infidelity as a ground for legal separation (Art. 55(8)). Tolentino explains why law and public opinion treat the wife's infidelity more seriously: it may introduce illegitimate children into the family. From the strictly ideal point of view, "there can be no difference" between the two. (The unequal penal treatment is criticized today, but it remains the RPC rule unless amended.)
Tolentino (mutual love and respect): the 1870 Marriage Law's "the wife must obey her husband" was abolished, and mutual respect is imposed on both spouses, reflecting their equality. Love between spouses (added by the FC) "cannot be compelled or imposed by court action". If affection cools and the "little things" are forgotten, neither can go to court. But when the disappearance of love shows itself in repeated violence or grossly abusive conduct, including serious insults, the remedy is legal separation (Art. 55).
Tolentino (position of the spouses, standard of conduct): the old doctrine of marital authority yielded to a theory of "unity of direction" that gives the husband some power of decision as head of the family, which consists of duties more than prerogatives. In matters purely personal to the wife, the husband cannot command or prohibit her: her relations with children of a former marriage or close relatives, her studies, her dress, smoking or dancing, legitimate friendships. He cannot open her letters to or from close relatives or intercept her business correspondence. Chastisement of the wife is unlawful. The relations of husband and wife are confidential, like trustee and cestui que trust: each must deal with the other in the highest good faith. On contracts between spouses, Tolentino argues that the old incapacity of married women no longer exists, so spouses may contract with each other except where a specific law prohibits it (e.g., sale between spouses under Art. 1490 NCC; donations under Art. 87 FC).
Note (outdated views): Tolentino's passages on the husband's "reasonable control of his wife's person" and correspondence, and on the husband as "head of the family", were written under a husband-centered tradition. The FC itself abolished the husband's predominance in fixing residence (Art. 69), support (Art. 70), household management (Art. 71), the wife's profession (Art. 73), administration of property (Arts. 96, 124) and parental authority (Art. 211, joint). The 1987 Constitution (Art. II, Sec. 14) and the Magna Carta of Women (RA 9710) require equality. On an exam, state the FC rule: the spouses are equals, and no spouse has a unilateral power of control over the other. Cite Tolentino only for the general principles (good faith, privacy of correspondence, no chastisement).

Tolentino: RPC 247 is the only (limited) exception to the rule that a spouse may not chastise or inflict violence on the other. Physical violence is otherwise a ground for legal separation (Art. 55(1)). Uribe's syllabus lists RPC 247 under "Obligation to live together" (with RPC 11(2) and FC 72).

5. Mutual help and support

Tolentino (mutual help): each spouse, in his or her own sphere, must contribute to the purposes of the family. This includes care during sickness and bearing the inconvenience it brings. Under the Siete Partidas, if a spouse becomes blind, deaf, deformed, a leper or ill, the other may not desert him or her. The duty is not limited to material assistance; it covers "moral assistance, and mutual affection and regard." Positive provisions that implement it: (1) legitimate defense of a spouse (RPC 11(2)); (2) the higher penalty for crimes by one spouse against the other (parricide, RPC 246); (3) the marital disqualification rule (one spouse cannot testify against the other, with exceptions, Rules of Court); (4) the right of one spouse to object to adoption by or of the other (Tolentino cites FC 185, 188; the FC adoption articles have since been superseded by special adoption laws, now RA 11642); (5) the prohibition of donations between spouses (Art. 87). Legal sanction: the only part of mutual help with a legal sanction is the duty to support, which can be enforced by court action. The law cannot reach the intimate relations of care and moral assistance; "there are no effective juridical means to compel compliance with them."

Art. 68 and psychological incapacity (Art. 36)

Republic v. Court of Appeals and Molina (G.R. No. 108763, February 13, 1997)

Doctrine (guideline 6): "The essential marital obligations must be those embraced by Articles 68 up to 71 of the Family Code as regards the husband and wife as well as Articles 220, 221 and 225 of the same Code in regard to parents and their children." These non-complied obligations must be stated in the petition, proven by evidence and included in the decision. Molina also teaches that the FC requires spouses "to live together, to observe mutual love, respect and fidelity, and render mutual help and support", and that failure to observe these roles "will most likely lead to the break-up of the marriage" (B29). The failure to live together may be attributed to psychological incapacity, but only if it springs from a true incapacity.

Note (current law): under Tan-Andal v. Andal (2021), psychological incapacity is a legal, not medical, concept. It must be shown by clear and convincing evidence of a durable or enduring aspect of a spouse's personality structure, existing at the time of the celebration, that makes him or her unable to understand and comply with the essential marital obligations. Expert testimony is not indispensable, and incurability is understood in a legal sense. Mere refusal, neglect, immaturity, infidelity or abandonment does not equal incapacity. These are breaches of Art. 68 remedied by legal separation, separation of property or Art. 72 relief. The essential obligations are still those in Arts. 68–71 and 220, 221, 225.
Quiz trap (bank L-108): "The essential marital obligations contemplated in Article 36 include those embraced by Articles 68 up to 71." This is TRUE (Molina). Watch for "Arts. 68 to 73" (wrong: 72–73 are not listed) or for the omission of the parent–child articles (220, 221, 225).

Fidelity and the pendency of legal separation

The filing of a petition for legal separation only entitles the spouses to live separately (Art. 61). The decree does not sever the bond (Art. 63(1)). Therefore the duty of fidelity continues, and adultery or concubinage committed during the case or after the decree remains punishable. Only the duty to live together is suspended.

Art. 61, FC (par. 1). After the filing of the petition for legal separation, the spouses shall be entitled to live separately from each other.
Quiz trap (bank L-92): "The obligation of mutual fidelity between the spouses ceases upon the filing of a petition for legal separation." This is FALSE. Only cohabitation is suspended (Art. 61); the marriage bond and fidelity remain.

W left the conjugal home in Quezon City and went back to her parents in Lucena because she was "tired" of H, who had done nothing wrong. H sues to compel her to return under pain of contempt, and alternatively asks the court to order the police to bring her home. Rule.

Answer: Deny both. Under Arroyo v. Vasquez de Arroyo, it is not within the province of the courts to compel a spouse to cohabit; no contempt-enforceable order may issue to restore the purely personal right of consortium. Neither can habeas corpus or police force be used (Ilusorio v. Bildner; Tolentino: our law has no counterpart of Argentine Art. 187). The court may at most declare her duty to return. H's remedies are patrimonial and indirect: he may refuse support because she left without just cause (Arts. 100(1)/127(1); Atilano), sue for legal separation if the abandonment without justifiable cause lasts more than one year (Art. 55(10)), and petition for receivership, judicial separation of property or sole administration (Arts. 101/128, 135(4)).

Same facts, but W left because H had been slapping her and demanding sexual acts she found degrading. W sues for support while living with her parents. H answers that he is ready to support her, but only if she returns home. Decide.

Answer: W wins. Under Goitia v. Campos-Rueda, a wife forced out of the conjugal home without fault on her part may obtain separate maintenance. H's option under Art. 204 to support her in the family dwelling "cannot be availed of in case there is a moral or legal obstacle thereto", and his maltreatment and demands are such an obstacle. She left with just cause, so Art. 100(1)/127(1) does not bar her. She need not first obtain legal separation. She may also seek a protection order and support under RA 9262 and, if a ground exists, legal separation (Art. 55(1)).

H promised in a notarized document to pay W ₱50,000 a month "for as long as W continues to live with me as my wife." W stayed for a year, then sued to collect. Is the promise enforceable?

Answer: No. The promise is void for want of cause or consideration. Cohabitation is something W is already bound by law to do (Art. 68), so doing it cannot be the consideration for a promise (Tolentino). (Support due under Arts. 195 and 70 is a different matter. It is owed by law, not by this contract.)

Art. 69: the family domicile

Art. 69, FC. The husband and wife shall fix the family domicile. In case of disagreement, the court shall decide.
The court may exempt one spouse from living with the other if the latter should live abroad or there are other valid and compelling reasons for the exemption. However, such exemption shall not apply if the same is not compatible with the solidarity of the family. (110a)
Art. 110, Civil Code [repealed]. The husband shall fix the residence of the family. But the court may exempt the wife from living with the husband if he should live abroad unless in the service of the Republic.
Art. 50, Civil Code. For the exercise of civil rights and the fulfillment of civil obligations, the domicile of natural persons is the place of their habitual residence.

Rule → exception → exception to the exception

  • General rule (par. 1): the husband and wife jointly fix the family domicile.
  • If they disagree: the court decides. Not the husband, not the family council, not the parents.
  • Exception, exemption from living together (par. 2): the court may exempt one spouse from living with the other if (a) the latter lives abroad, or (b) there are other valid and compelling reasons.
  • Exception to the exception: the exemption "shall not apply if the same is not compatible with the solidarity of the family."

Tolentino (Art. 69):
  • Joint right. This is a logical consequence of the mutual obligation to live together. In case of disagreement, either spouse can petition the court.
  • Change of domicile. The right to fix includes the right to change, provided the spouses agree on the transfer. If not, either or both may go to court. A valid reason to move might be that the neighborhood has become a commercial, noisy, night-life or gambling area. But such circumstances "should not justify a spouse to separate and live apart from the other"; the remedy is to go to court "so as to preserve the solidarity of the family."

Example: the in-laws' house becomes unbearable, or the original location makes it impossible to continue a business or profession. Instead of asking the court to transfer the residence, the spouse may be allowed to live separately.
  • Living abroad. The Civil Code had no express provision, but the court had discretion to exempt the wife from following the husband abroad for just cause. Grounds accepted: an officially declared epidemic in the foreign country; war there; danger of abandonment of the wife's separate property; sickness of the wife or a child; the impossibility of the children continuing their studies; the transfer being in compliance with a penalty.

"We believe that in spite of the provision in this article, it is still discretionary for the court to exempt or not a spouse from the obligation to live with the other abroad." Just grounds include the above, or where living abroad would force a spouse to give up a business, profession or public office in the Philippines. Grounds for refusing the exemption: e.g., the spouse who does not want to go abroad may be involved in illicit relations here. And even if not exempted, the spouse "cannot be compelled to follow the other abroad, but must suffer the consequences, such as loss of the right to support."

Abella v. COMELEC (201 SCRA 253, September 3, 1991)

Larrazabal ran for governor of Leyte claiming residence in Kananga, Leyte. The COMELEC found that she had established her residence in Ormoc City with her husband, and disqualified her; the Supreme Court sustained the disqualification. Doctrine: "Mere absence from one's residence or origin—domicile—to pursue studies, engage in business, or practice his avocation, is not sufficient to constitute abandonment or loss of such residence." The determination of a person's legal residence or domicile "largely depends upon intention which may be inferred from his acts, activities and utterances". The party claiming abandonment must prove it by a preponderance of evidence. Applied: there was no proof that she left Kananga temporarily to pursue a calling, profession or business. What was clear was that she had established her residence in Ormoc City with her husband and considered herself a resident there. Uribe lists it under FC 68–69 (domicile of natural persons, NCC 50).

Note (related doctrine, Romualdez-Marcos v. COMELEC, 1995): a wife does not automatically lose her domicile of origin by marriage. Under the old Civil Code, the husband fixed the family "residence", which is not the same as the wife's legal domicile. Under Art. 69 FC the family domicile is fixed jointly. Domicile requires physical presence plus intent to remain (animus manendi), and a change requires (1) actual removal, (2) a bona fide intention to abandon the old domicile and establish a new one, and (3) acts consistent with that purpose. No minimum period of physical presence is an element of a change of domicile (bank P-88).
Quiz trap: Art. 50 NCC says "habitual residence", not "actual residence" (bank P-86). Art. 69 says "family domicile". Art. 110 NCC said "residence of the family". Art. 69's exemption is permissive ("the court may exempt"); it is never automatic (bank L-95).

H, a career diplomat, is posted to an embassy in Europe. W refuses to go, because she runs a business in Manila and the children study here. H asks the court to require W to live with him abroad, invoking Art. 110 of the Civil Code ("unless in the service of the Republic"). Decide.

Answer: Art. 110 is repealed. Under Art. 69, the court may exempt W from living with H because H lives abroad, unless the exemption is not compatible with the solidarity of the family. The FC dropped the "service of the Republic" exception, so H's being in government service no longer automatically defeats the exemption. The matter is discretionary (Tolentino): W's business and the children's schooling are recognized valid reasons. Even if no exemption is granted, the court cannot order W, under pain of contempt, to go abroad. At most she suffers patrimonial consequences such as loss of support if her refusal is unjustified.

After the wedding, H and W lived in H's parents' house. W cannot get along with her mother-in-law and constantly quarrels with her. H refuses to move out. W wants to live separately but does not want legal separation. Remedy?

Answer: Since the spouses disagree on the family domicile, W may ask the court to decide (Art. 69, par. 1). Alternatively she may ask the court to exempt her from living with H for "other valid and compelling reasons" (par. 2), unless this is incompatible with family solidarity. Tolentino gives this exact example: she may be allowed to live separately, and a spouse lawfully living apart keeps the right to support. She may also invoke Art. 72 for relief.

Art. 70: support of the family; order of liability

Art. 70, FC. The spouses are jointly responsible for the support of the family. The expenses for such support and other conjugal obligations shall be paid from the community property and, in the absence thereof, from the income or fruits of their separate properties. In case of insufficiency or absence of said income or fruits, such obligations shall be satisfied from the separate properties. (111a)

Order of payment (memorize the sequence)

  1. Community property (ACP), or conjugal partnership property (CPG);
  2. in its absence, the income or fruits of the spouses' separate properties;
  3. in case of insufficiency or absence of said income or fruits, the separate properties themselves.
Quiz trap (bank L-89, L-97, L-98): (a) "first from the separate properties, then community" is FALSE; (b) "in the absence of community property, directly from the separate properties, the income or fruits being reached only if insufficient" is FALSE, because fruits come before capital; (c) "exclusively by the husband as head of the family" is the Civil Code idea and FALSE; (d) the spouses are "jointly responsible", not "the husband is primarily responsible". Also: the provision covers "support of the family and other conjugal obligations".
Tolentino (Arts. 70–71): under the Civil Code (Art. 111) the husband was responsible for the support of the wife and the rest of the family. The FC made it a joint responsibility, "a consequence of the joint rights of the spouses under the Family Code in many areas of family life in which the husband, under the Civil Code, used to enjoy predominance" (family residence, management of the conjugal partnership, parental authority). Tolentino adds, critically: "This change is logically correct, but it is doubtful if it is in line with what has already developed as a national custom in family relations among Filipinos." What properties answer: first the community property, then the income or fruits of the separate properties, finally the separate properties. Because the liability is "joint", it might mean equal contribution regardless of the value of each spouse's separate property. "However, this would not be equitable. The better rule seems to be that the contribution should be proportionate to the properties of the spouses."

Art. 71: management of the household

Art. 71, FC. The management of the household shall be the right and the duty of both spouses. The expenses for such management shall be paid in accordance with the provisions of Article 70. (115a)
  • "Right and duty" of "both" spouses. Under the Civil Code (Art. 115) the wife managed the affairs of the household.
  • Expenses: paid per Art. 70 (community → income/fruits of separate properties → separate properties).
  • The provision does not say what happens on disagreement; unlike Arts. 69 and 73, it has no "court shall decide" clause.
Tolentino: making household management the right and duty of both spouses "is carrying the principle of equality of the spouses a little too far." If they disagree, how is it settled? "The law is silent on this point." Filipino custom considers the wife the "queen of the home", who traditionally runs the household and holds the family purse even if she does not contribute to it. Given the law's silence, Tolentino says "the custom should be observed, and the wife's position should be given priority" (cf. Art. 9 NCC, on courts deciding despite the silence of the law). The law also does not preclude an agreement that only one spouse manages the household, e.g., the husband may agree to management by the wife.
Note: the bank explanation for L-90 omits Tolentino's custom-based suggestion. The suggestion is Tolentino's personal view (p. 351), not a codal rule. In a T/F item, the codal answer controls: "management of the household shall be the right and duty of the wife" is FALSE (it is "both"). "In case of disagreement the court shall decide" is not in Art. 71 (bank L-90), so mark such a statement false.
Quiz trap (bank L-98): "The management of the household shall be the right and the duty of both spouses, and the expenses for such management shall be paid first from the separate properties." This is FALSE: expenses follow Art. 70, so community property comes first.

Related: household work as contribution. In property disputes (Arts. 147–148 and liquidation), the care and maintenance of the family and household are recognized as contributions (Art. 147 par. 2: a party who did not participate in the acquisition is deemed to have contributed jointly if his or her efforts consisted in the care and maintenance of the family and of the household). This shows the law's valuation of household management, but those articles belong to the property relations topic.

Art. 72: judicial relief against a spouse who neglects duties or endangers the family

Art. 72, FC. When one of the spouses neglects his or her duties to the conjugal union or commits acts which tend to bring danger, dishonor or injury to the other or to the family, the aggrieved party may apply to the court for relief. (116a)

Elements

  1. A valid, subsisting marriage (the provision speaks of "spouses" and "conjugal union");
  2. One spouse either (a) neglects his or her duties to the conjugal union, or (b) commits acts which tend to bring danger, dishonor or injury to the other or to the family. Actual harm is not required; "tend to" suffices.
  3. The aggrieved party applies to the court;
  4. For relief. The kind of relief is not specified, but it must be lawful.
Quiz trap: the triad is "danger, dishonor or injury". The NCC (Art. 116) spoke of "danger, dishonor or material injury". The persons protected are "the other or to the family". The relief-seeker is "the aggrieved party", who "may apply to the court". Art. 72 does not require a prior legal separation, does not require a criminal conviction, and does not require a ground under Art. 55 (bank L-100: Art. 72 is an independent remedy; there is no family council requirement).
Tolentino (what relief may be granted): under the Civil Code, when a spouse applied for relief, "the court may counsel the offender to comply with his or her duties, and take such measures as may be proper." The FC suppressed this sentence. Tolentino nonetheless believes "the court has full freedom to determine the kind of relief that may be given. The relief, however, must be lawful."
Tolentino (does Art. 72 overrule Arroyo?): Dean Capistrano, critical of Arroyo, wrote: "Perhaps in the near future a wiser and more progressive Supreme Court could in a similar case, grant a more adequate and effective relief." Tolentino disagrees: "We believe that the Arroyo doctrine still stands, in so far as it refuses to use the force of public authority, bolstered by contempt proceedings, to compel cohabitation between the spouses." His reasons: (1) if the Code meant to abrogate Arroyo it would have said so explicitly; (2) the codifiers borrowed heavily from the Argentine Code yet did not copy its Art. 187 (police force to compel cohabitation); (3) "measures as may be proper" means measures provided by law for the particular breach, not remedies the judge creates on his own discretion, which "would be a delegation of legislative power"; (4) coercive force in the internal aspect of the family may be more harmful than beneficial.

Examples of lawful relief (Tolentino)

  • Breach of the duty of living together by the husband: the court cannot compel him to return. It can counsel him to do so. If he still refuses without just cause, then on the wife's petition the court may (1) order payment of separate maintenance to the wife, an order enforceable by execution and contempt (the support order is patrimonial, so contempt is allowed here); (2) grant separation of property or receivership of the conjugal property, or legal separation if the abandonment lasts more than a year; and (3) grant judicial authorization for acts of the wife requiring the husband's consent.
  • Breach of mutual respect (continual insults): if, after counselling, the husband continues his unbearable attitude, the wife may separate and the court will grant her separate maintenance.
  • Extreme cases (maltreatment and injury): the court may order prosecution for physical injuries or attempted parricide, and the wife may ask for legal separation.
  • General principle: "The coercive power of the court may be made to affect the patrimony of the offending spouse; it may even impose penalties if the offense constitutes a crime; but it cannot be used to make him perform a personal act."
Reviewer note in Uribe materials (2013 notes): "The injury contemplated is not economic or financial injury but physical, moral, emotional or psychological injury." Note, however, that the first limb ("neglects his or her duties to the conjugal union") easily covers economic neglect, such as failure to support. Economic abuse is also expressly covered by RA 9262 and Arts. 101/128. For a T/F item on Art. 72, rely on the codal triad.

Art. 72 and its companion provisions (relief options)

Art. 101, FC. If a spouse without just cause abandons the other or fails to comply with his or her obligations to the family, the aggrieved spouse may petition the court for receivership, for judicial separation of property or for authority to be the sole administrator of the absolute community, subject to such precautionary conditions as the court may impose.
The obligations to the family mentioned in the preceding paragraph refer to marital, parental or property relations.
A spouse is deemed to have abandoned the other when he or she has left the conjugal dwelling without intention of returning. The spouse who has left the conjugal dwelling for a period of three months or has failed within the same period to give any information as to his or her whereabouts shall be prima facie presumed to have no intention of returning to the conjugal dwelling.

Art. 128 is the CPG counterpart of Art. 101 (receivership, judicial separation of property, or sole administration of the conjugal partnership; the same three-month presumption). Art. 135 lists the sufficient causes for judicial separation of property: (1) civil interdiction of the other spouse; (2) the other spouse has been judicially declared an absentee; (3) loss of parental authority decreed by the court; (4) the other spouse has abandoned the petitioner or failed to comply with obligations to the family as provided in Art. 101; (5) abuse of the power of administration granted in the marriage settlements; (6) separation in fact for at least one year with reconciliation highly improbable.

Quiz trap (bank L-106): the three-month presumption (Art. 101 par. 3) is only a prima facie presumption of intent not to return for purposes of Art. 101/128 remedies. Abandonment as a ground for legal separation requires abandonment without justifiable cause for more than one year (Art. 55(10)). "Absence for three months is, by itself, a ground for legal separation" is FALSE. Likewise, "abandonment for more than one year is a ground for legal separation" without "without justifiable cause" is not absolutely true (bank L-3, L-20).
Quiz trap (bank L-84, constructive abandonment): a husband who forcibly ejects his wife from the conjugal dwelling without just cause and refuses to take her back commits constructive abandonment, even though she is the one who physically left. She is not the deserting spouse and keeps her right to support. (Cf. De la Viña v. Villareal (1920): the wife may acquire a separate domicile when the husband unlawfully ejects her or gives cause for separation.) Recrimination is a defense in legal separation; de facto separation is merely the fact of living apart.

H habitually gambles away the family's money and leaves W and the children without food. W does not want legal separation, because she hopes H will reform. What can she do?

Answer: W may apply to the court for relief under Art. 72: H neglects his duties to the conjugal union and his acts tend to bring injury to the family. Art. 72 is independent of legal separation. Lawful relief includes an order of support (enforceable by execution and contempt), and under Art. 101 (failure to comply with obligations to the family, here property and parental), receivership, judicial separation of property (also Art. 135(4)) or sole administration of the community. She may also invoke RA 9262 (economic abuse) for a protection order directing support. The court cannot imprison H to force him to be a good husband, but it can reach his patrimony (Tolentino).

Under Art. 72, may the court order an erring husband who moved out to resume cohabitation with the aggrieved wife under pain of contempt?

Answer: No (bank L-99). Art. 72 allows only lawful relief, and Tolentino says the Arroyo doctrine survives: the court's coercive power may affect the offending spouse's patrimony or punish a crime, but cannot compel a purely personal act such as cohabitation.

Art. 73: exercise of a profession, occupation, business or activity (as amended by RA 10572)

Art. 73, FC (as amended by RA 10572, 2013). Either spouse may exercise any legitimate profession, occupation, business or activity without the consent of the other. The latter may object only on valid, serious, and moral grounds.
In case of disagreement, the court shall decide whether or not:
(1) The objection is proper, and
(2) Benefit has accrued to the family prior to the objection or thereafter. If the benefit accrued prior to the objection, the resulting obligation shall be enforced against the community property. If the benefit accrued thereafter, such obligation shall be enforced against the separate property of the spouse who has not obtained consent.
The foregoing provisions shall not prejudice the rights of creditors who acted in good faith.

Structure: rule → limit → court → consequences → creditor protection

  1. Rule: either spouse may exercise any legitimate profession, occupation, business or activity without the consent of the other.
  2. Limit: the other spouse may object, but only on valid, serious, and moral grounds (all three).
  3. Disagreement: the court decides two questions: (1) whether the objection is proper; and (2) whether benefit accrued to the family prior to the objection or thereafter.
  4. Consequences for obligations from the activity:
    • benefit accrued prior to the objection → resulting obligation is enforced against the community property;
    • benefit accrued after the objection → enforced against the separate property of the spouse who has not obtained consent.
  5. Proviso: none of this prejudices creditors who acted in good faith, i.e., those without knowledge of the objection or of the absence of consent.

Tolentino (Art. 73): Source: Art. 117 NCC, itself from Art. 167 of the Swiss Code and Arts. 169–171 of the Mexican Code. "cf. NCC 113": suits by or against a spouse

Old Art. 113 NCC: "The husband must be joined in all suits by or against the wife, except: (1) When they are judicially separated; (2) If they have in fact been separated for at least one year; (3) When there is a separation of property agreed upon in the marriage settlements; (4) If the administration of all the property in the marriage has been transferred to her...; (5) When the litigation is between the husband and wife; (6) If the suit concerns her paraphernal property; (7) When the action is upon the civil liability arising from a criminal offense; (8) If the litigation is incidental to the profession, occupation or business in which she is engaged; (9) In any civil action referred to in articles 25 to 35; and (10) In an action upon a quasi-delict. In the cases mentioned in Nos. 7 to 10, the husband must be joined as a party defendant if the third paragraph of article 163 is applicable." The FC has no counterpart. The present procedural rule is Rule 3, Sec. 4, Rules of Court: "Husband and wife shall sue or be sued jointly, except as provided by law." Uribe pairs NCC 113 with Art. 73 because exception (8), litigation incidental to the wife's profession or business, foreshadowed the wife's independent capacity in her own work. Related: Art. 111 FC (as amended), under which a spouse may dispose of exclusive property and "appear alone in court to litigate with regard to the same."

Note: old NCC 114 (the wife could not, without the husband's consent, acquire property by gratuitous title except from certain relatives) is also repealed. The FC has no such restriction.

Art. 73 and the property regime

  • Under ACP/CPG, income from the profession or business is community or conjugal property (Art. 91; Art. 117(2): "those obtained from the labor, industry, work or profession of either or both of the spouses").
  • Debts and obligations contracted by the designated administrator-spouse for the benefit of the community, by both, or by one with the other's consent, are chargeable to the ACP/CPG (Arts. 94(2), 121(2)). Debts contracted by either spouse without the other's consent are chargeable only to the extent that the family may have been benefited (Arts. 94(3), 121(3)). Art. 73 is the specific application of that "benefit" test to obligations from a profession or business that met an objection.

W, a nurse, opened a small online selling business in 2022 without H's knowledge. Its profits paid for the children's tuition in 2022–2023. In January 2024 H formally objected, on the ground that the business sells counterfeit goods and exposes the family to criminal liability. W continued. In mid-2024 she contracted a ₱400,000 supplier debt. The supplier did not know of H's objection. Rule on (1) whether H's objection is proper, (2) which property answers for obligations, and (3) the supplier's position.

Answer: (1) Art. 73 protects only a legitimate activity. Selling counterfeit goods is not legitimate, and the objection rests on valid, serious and moral grounds, so the court should find it proper. (2) Obligations whose benefit accrued to the family before the January 2024 objection are enforced against the community property. Obligations whose benefit accrued after it are enforced against W's separate property, since she is the spouse who has not obtained consent. (3) The supplier, having acted in good faith (no knowledge of the objection), is protected: "the foregoing provisions shall not prejudice the rights of creditors who acted in good faith." Its claim is not defeated by the internal allocation between the spouses.

H objects to W's becoming a nightclub singer because "his income as a lawyer is sufficient for the family's social standing" and he prefers that she stay home. W insists. Does H's objection prevail?

Answer: Not on those grounds alone. "Sufficiency of the husband's income" was a requisite under the repealed Art. 117 NCC; it is not a ground under Art. 73. Under Art. 73, W needs no consent, and H may object only on valid, serious and moral grounds. A mere preference is not enough. If H shows, for instance, that the work exposes W to dangers to honor and reputation or to an immoral environment (Tolentino's examples), the court may find the objection proper. Disagreements go to the court, not to the parents, grandparents or a family council.

Separation in fact (Arts. 100 and 127)

Art. 100, FC. The separation in fact between husband and wife shall not affect the regime of absolute community except that:
(1) The spouse who leaves the conjugal home or refuses to live therein, without just cause, shall not have the right to be supported;
(2) When the consent of one spouse to any transaction of the other is required by law, judicial authorization shall be obtained in a summary proceeding;
(3) In the absence of sufficient community property, the separate property of both spouses shall be solidarily liable for the support of the family. The spouse present shall, upon proper petition in a summary proceeding, be given judicial authority to administer or encumber any specific separate property of the other spouse and use the fruits or proceeds thereof to satisfy the latter's share.
Art. 127, FC. The separation in fact between husband and wife shall not affect the regime of conjugal partnership, except that:
(1) The spouse who leaves the conjugal home or refuses to live therein, without just cause, shall not have the right to be supported;
(2) When the consent of one spouse to any transaction of the other is required by law, judicial authorization shall be obtained in a summary proceeding;
(3) In the absence of sufficient conjugal partnership property, the separate property of both spouses shall be solidarily liable for the support of the family. The spouse present shall, upon petition in a summary proceeding, be given judicial authority to administer or encumber any specific separate property of the other spouse and use the fruits or proceeds thereof to satisfy the latter's share.
  • General rule: separation in fact does not dissolve or alter the property regime, and does not affect the marriage bond or the duties of fidelity and respect.
  • Three exceptions: (1) loss of support by the spouse who leaves or refuses to live in the conjugal home without just cause (codifies Atilano); (2) the absent spouse's consent is replaced by judicial authorization in a summary proceeding (not dispensed with); (3) solidary liability of both spouses' separate properties for family support, with authority for the present spouse to administer or encumber the other's specific separate property.
  • Separation in fact for at least one year with reconciliation highly improbable is a ground for judicial separation of property (Art. 135(6)). It is not, by itself, a ground for legal separation (compare Art. 55(10), which requires abandonment without justifiable cause for more than one year).
  • Procedure: the summary judicial proceedings in the FC (Title XI, Arts. 238–253; Art. 239 is the petition when spouses are separated in fact or one has abandoned the other and consent is withheld or cannot be obtained). The judgment is immediately final and executory.
Quiz trap (bank L-104, L-105): "In case of separation in fact, when the consent of one spouse is required by law, such consent is dispensed with." FALSE: judicial authorization in a summary proceeding is required. "In separation in fact with insufficient community property, only the spouse who left is liable from his separate property" is FALSE: the separate properties of both are solidarily liable. "The obligation to support is suspended during separation in fact" is FALSE.
Reviewer summary in Uribe materials (summary proceedings when spouses are separated in fact or one abandoned the other): a verified petition; claims for damages (except costs) must be litigated in a separate action; jurisdiction lies with the family court or RTC where either spouse resides; notice to the other spouse to show cause, served at the last known address; a preliminary conference conducted personally by the judge without counsel; ex parte proceedings if the non-consenting spouse does not appear despite efforts; summary hearing on affidavits, documents or oral testimony; the judgment is immediately final and executory (certiorari to the SC for grave abuse). The same rules govern a petition for authority to administer or encumber the abandoning spouse's separate property for family support.

RA 9262 (Anti-VAWC Act) and other laws reinforcing the spousal duties

The Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004 (RA 9262) turns many breaches of Art. 68 into acts of violence against a wife or former wife (and her children), with criminal liability and protection orders (barangay, temporary and permanent). Points to know for this section:

  • Forms of violence: physical, sexual, psychological and economic. Economic abuse includes depriving or threatening to deprive the woman or her children of financial support legally due them. Psychological violence includes acts causing mental or emotional suffering, and marital infidelity can be a means of committing it (B29 lists Araza v. People: psychological violence may be executed through marital infidelity causing emotional anguish and mental suffering).
  • Protection orders may direct the respondent to stay away, to leave the residence, to provide support, and the like. These are statutory reliefs that go beyond Art. 72's general "relief", but even they do not compel cohabitation.
  • Persons liable: the offender must have a marital, sexual or dating relationship with the woman, but others may be liable through conspiracy under the RPC applied suppletorily (Go-Tan v. Spouses Tan, 2008: the parents-in-law were included in the protection order).
  • Support is mutual: RA 9262 protects women but does not discount their capacity to support themselves; "the obligation to provide support is imposed by the law mutually upon both spouses" (B29, XXX v. People).
  • Legal separation interface: RA 9262, Sec. 19: "In cases of legal separation, where violence as specified in this Act is alleged, Article 58 of the Family Code shall not apply. The court shall proceed on the main case and other incidents of the case as soon as possible" (bank L-80).
  • Battered woman syndrome is a defense under RA 9262 (Sec. 26). It is mentioned here only as part of the protective framework.

Other laws giving effect to the spousal duties: RPC 333–334 (adultery and concubinage, which sanction fidelity); RPC 246 (parricide); RPC 11(2) (defense of spouse); RPC 247 (exceptional circumstances); RA 8353 (marital rape); NCC 26 (interference with family relations); NCC 921(4) (disinheritance of a spouse who has given cause for legal separation); Rule 3, Sec. 4 (joinder of spouses); the marital disqualification and marital privilege rules on evidence; Art. 87 FC (void donations between spouses during marriage, except moderate gifts on family rejoicing); NCC 1490 (sale between spouses prohibited except under separation of property).

Note: the spouses' duties under Art. 68 bind both spouses equally, but RA 9262 protects only women (and their children) as victims. A husband who is abused must rely on the FC (Arts. 55, 72, 101, 135), the RPC and civil damages.

W discovers H's serious affair with his secretary, which H refuses to end. W does not want to end the marriage, but wants to protect the family's assets. What remedies are available: (a) nullity, (b) annulment, (c) legal separation, (d) separation of property?

Answer: (c) legal separation (sexual infidelity, Art. 55(8)) and/or (d) judicial separation of property for failure to comply with marital obligations (Art. 135(4) in relation to Arts. 68 and 101), plus Art. 72 relief. Infidelity arising during the marriage is not a ground for nullity or annulment, and sexual infidelity per se is not psychological incapacity (bank L-49). Since W wants to keep the marriage, separation of property and Art. 72 fit best. She may also invoke RA 9262 if the infidelity causes her mental or emotional anguish.

H beats W every time he comes home drunk. Which of the four remedies (nullity, annulment, legal separation, separation of property) are available?

Answer: Legal separation (repeated physical violence, Art. 55(1)); judicial separation of property (Art. 135(4) with Arts. 68 and 101, breach of mutual respect); and declaration of nullity only if the violent behavior manifests a psychological incapacity existing at the time of the celebration (Art. 36). "Force" under Art. 45(4) refers to force used to obtain consent to the marriage, so annulment is unavailable (bank L-50). Add Art. 72 relief and an RA 9262 protection order.

Last-minute checklist

  • Art. 68 = 5 duties: live together; mutual love (new in the FC), respect, fidelity; mutual help and support. Source: NCC 109. "Obliged".
  • Cohabitation is not enforceable by compulsion: no contempt, injunction or mandamus (Arroyo 1921; Ramirez-Cuaderno 1964), no habeas corpus (Ilusorio 2000), no police force (Tolentino). Court may only admonish. Sanctions are patrimonial.
  • Left without just cause → no support (Atilano 1958; Arts. 100(1)/127(1)). Left with just cause → separate maintenance outside the home (Goitia 1916; Art. 204's "moral or legal obstacle").
  • Wrongful desertion and refusal of consortium → damages (Tenchavez). Third-party interference → Art. 26 NCC.
  • Promise to pay the wife for cohabitation or sex → void (no cause). Sexual right not absolute; marital rape punishable (RA 8353).
  • Molina: essential obligations = Arts. 68–71 + 220, 221, 225. Tan-Andal: refusal ≠ incapacity.
  • Filing of LS petition → live separately (Art. 61); fidelity continues; bond subsists even after the decree.
  • Art. 69: husband and wife fix the family domicile; disagreement → court. Court may exempt one spouse if the other lives abroad or for other valid and compelling reasons; not if incompatible with the solidarity of the family. Exemption is discretionary, never automatic.
  • NCC 110: the husband fixed the residence; only the wife could be exempted, if he lived abroad, "unless in the service of the Republic" (dropped by the FC).
  • NCC 50: domicile = place of habitual residence. Abella: intent plus acts; absence for study, work or business ≠ abandonment; preponderance of evidence to prove abandonment.
  • Art. 70: spouses jointly responsible; order: community property → income/fruits of separate property → separate property. Tolentino: contribution should be proportionate to properties.
  • ACP/CPG insufficient → spouses solidarily liable with separate properties (Arts. 94, 121 last pars.). Complete separation → in proportion to income, then to current market value of separate property; liability to creditors solidary (Art. 146).
  • Art. 71: household management is the right and duty of both; expenses per Art. 70; no "court decides" clause (Tolentino: custom favors the wife; spouses may agree that one manages). NCC 115: the wife managed.
  • Art. 72: neglects duties to the conjugal union or acts that tend to bring danger, dishonor or injury to the other or to the family → aggrieved party may apply to the court for relief. Relief must be lawful; Arroyo still stands (Tolentino). The NCC 116 "counsel the offender" clause was dropped.
  • Art. 101/128: abandonment = left without intention of returning; 3 months away or no information on whereabouts → prima facie presumption; remedies are receivership, judicial separation of property, sole administration; "obligations to the family" = marital, parental or property.
  • LS ground: abandonment without justifiable cause for more than one year (Art. 55(10)). JSP ground: separated in fact at least one year + reconciliation highly improbable (Art. 135(6)).
  • Separation in fact (Arts. 100/127): regime unaffected except (1) the one who left without just cause loses support; (2) consent → judicial authorization, summary proceeding; (3) separate properties of both solidarily liable for family support.
  • Art. 73 (RA 10572, 2013): either spouse, any legitimate profession, occupation, business or activity, without consent; objection only on valid, serious, and moral grounds; the court decides (1) whether the objection is proper and (2) whether benefit accrued before or after the objection.
  • Benefit before the objection → community property; benefit after → separate property of the spouse who has not obtained consent; creditors in good faith are not prejudiced. (The 1987 text charged pre-objection obligations to the separate property; answer per the amended text.)
  • NCC 117: only the wife's work; husband may object if (1) his income is sufficient for the family's social standing and (2) serious and valid grounds; consult parents, grandparents, family council, then the court. All dropped by the FC.
  • Tolentino: the objection is a "conditional veto" based on unity of direction. Products of the activity: free disposal in the course of the activity; real estate etc. → spouses act jointly (common property).
  • Syllabus IX-D: Const. Art. II §14 (equality of women and men) and Art. XIII §14 (working women); cf. NCC 113 (husband joined in wife's suits; 10 exceptions) → now Rule 3 §4 (sue or be sued jointly except as provided by law).
  • Syllabus IX-A also lists RPC 11(2) (defense of spouse, a justifying circumstance) and RPC 247 (killing or serious injuries on surprising the spouse in sexual intercourse → destierro; other injuries → exempt; not available to one who promoted or consented to the infidelity).
  • RA 9262: economic abuse (deprivation of support), psychological violence (infidelity may qualify), protection orders; Sec. 19 means no 6-month cooling-off in LS if VAWC is alleged; protects women only; support is still mutual.
  • Mutual support between spouses ceases on death and after final judgment of nullity or annulment (Art. 198). In LS the court may order the guilty spouse to support the innocent spouse.
  • Syllabus cases to name: Atilano v. Chua Ching Beng; Goitia v. Campos-Rueda; Arroyo v. Vasquez de Arroyo; Ramirez-Cuaderno v. Cuaderno; Abella v. COMELEC (plus Ilusorio, Tenchavez, Molina).
