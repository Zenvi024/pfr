Absence

Civil Code, Title XIV, Arts. 381–396, with FC 41 (syllabus XX). The three stages of absence (provisional measures, declaration of absence, presumption of death), administration of the absentee's property, the 7/10/5/4-year rules, the absentee's reappearance, and contingent rights. Cases: Reyes v. Alejandro, Eastern Shipping v. Lucero, Tol-Noquera v. Villamor; from the class reviewer, Jones v. Hortiguela, Valmonte v. Villaroman. (The Persons section has a short summary of absence as a modification of capacity.)

Provisional measures (Arts. 381–383)

Art. 381, NCC. When a person disappears from his domicile, his whereabouts being unknown, and without leaving an agent to administer his property, the judge, at the instance of an interested party, a relative, or a friend, may appoint a person to represent him in all that may be necessary.
This same rule shall be observed when under similar circumstances the power conferred by the absentee has expired. (181a)
Art. 382, NCC. The appointment referred to in the preceding article having been made, the judge shall take the necessary measures to safeguard the rights and interests of the absentee and shall specify the powers, obligations and remuneration of his representative, regulating them, according to the circumstances, by the rules concerning guardians. (182)
Art. 383, NCC. In the appointment of a representative, the spouse present shall be preferred when there is no legal separation.
If the absentee left no spouse, or if the spouse present is a minor, any competent person may be appointed by the court. (183a)
Tolentino (Art. 383): unless the wife has been appointed administratrix or trustee of her absent husband's property, she cannot sue to recover it. The petition to declare the husband an absentee and the petition to place the conjugal property under the wife's management may be combined. Garrido v. North Camarines Lumber Co. (C.A.): a guerrilla's employer paid his unpaid prewar salaries to his wife in Japanese military notes during the occupation; the payment did not bind him, because the wife had never been judicially appointed his representative.

Declaration of absence (Arts. 384–386)

Art. 384, NCC. Two years having elapsed without any news about the absentee or since the receipt of the last news, and five years in case the absentee has left a person in charge of the administration of his property, his absence may be declared. (184)
Art. 385, NCC. The following may ask for the declaration of absence:
(1) The spouse present;
(2) The heirs instituted in a will, who may present an authentic copy of the same;
(3) The relatives who may succeed by the law of intestacy;
(4) Those who may have over the property of the absentee some right subordinated to the condition of his death. (185)
Art. 386, NCC. The judicial declaration of absence shall not take effect until six months after its publication in a newspaper of general circulation. (186a)
Tolentino (Art. 384): with no news at all, count from the disappearance; with news, count "not from the actual receipt of the news but from the time referred to by the news, or the time when the news was sent" (Jones v. Hortiguela). Why five years when he left an administrator: his affairs are attended to, so there is less urgency.
Reyes v. Alejandro (141 SCRA 65, 1986; the syllabus prints 41 SCRA)

A wife petitioned to have her husband, who had left the conjugal home years earlier, judicially declared an absentee; the petition alleged no property of his that needed administration. Held: dismissed. "The need to have a person judicially declared an absentee is when he has properties which have to be taken cared of or administered by a representative appointed by the Court (Article 384, Civil Code); the spouse of the absentee is asking for separation of property (Article 191, Civil Code) or his wife is asking the Court that the administration of all classes of property in the marriage be transferred to her (Article 196, Civil Code). The petition to declare the husband an absentee and the petition to place the management of the conjugal properties in the hands of the wife may be combined and adjudicated in the same proceedings" (Peyer v. Martinez). A declaration of absence is not needed merely to establish the fact of absence.

Jones v. Hortiguela (64 Phil. 179, 1937; class reviewer)

Marciana Escaño's first husband, Arthur Jones, left in 1918 and was not heard from; she obtained a judicial declaration of his absence (published, taking effect in 1921) and married Felix Hortiguela in 1927. The validity of the second marriage was later questioned. Held: valid. Under the then General Orders No. 68, a spouse could remarry after seven years of absence; "the requirement of the 7-year prescriptive period before a spouse may enter into a second marriage is counted from the date the last news concerning the absentee was received, not from the date of the publication of the judicial declaration of absence." From 1918 to 1927, more than seven years had passed.

Administration of the absentee's property (Arts. 387–389)

Art. 387, NCC. An administrator of the absentee's property shall be appointed in accordance with article 383. (187a)
Art. 388, NCC. The wife who is appointed as an administratrix of the husband's property cannot alienate or encumber the husband's property, or that of the conjugal partnership, without judicial authority. (188a)
Art. 389, NCC. The administration shall cease in any of the following cases:
(1) When the absentee appears personally or by means of an agent;
(2) When the death of the absentee is proved and his testate or intestate heirs appear;
(3) When a third person appears, showing by a proper document that he has acquired the absentee's property by purchase or other title.
In these cases the administrator shall cease in the performance of his office, and the property shall be at the disposal of those who may have a right thereto. (190)
Tol-Noquera v. Villamor (211 SCRA 616, 1992)

Remigio Tol disappeared. His daughter, Daya Maria Tol-Noquera, petitioned for the declaration of his absence and for appointment as administratrix of his estate. The trial court dismissed it because a lot he claimed was titled to someone else, and because she, as heir, might inherit the property. Held: reinstated. "It is not necessary that a declaration of absence be made in a proceeding separate from and prior to a petition for administration" (Reyes v. Alejandro; Peyer v. Martinez). "The purpose of the cited rules is the protection of the interests and property of the absentee, not of the administrator. Thus, the question of whether the administrator may inherit the property to be administered is not controlling. What is material is whether she is one of those allowed by law to seek the declaration of absence … and whether she is competent to be appointed as administratrix." Ownership of the disputed lot should be resolved in another proceeding; she may use reconveyance or damages if appointed.

Tolentino (Art. 388): there was a conflict between this article and old NCC 197 (which gave the wife administering all the marriage's property the husband's full powers); it was settled by the omission of NCC 196–197 from the Family Code, so Art. 388 stands: no alienation or encumbrance without judicial authority. Compare FC 96 and 124: a spouse who assumes sole administration because the other is "otherwise unable to participate" also needs court authority or the other's written consent to dispose or encumber; otherwise the transaction is void (but a continuing offer). Sale or encumbrance of the absentee's property follows Rule 98, Sec. 9, like a guardian's.

Presumption of death (Arts. 390–392; FC 41)

Art. 390, NCC. After an absence of seven years, it being unknown whether or not the absentee still lives, he shall be presumed dead for all purposes, except for those of succession.
The absentee shall not be presumed dead for the purpose of opening his succession till after an absence of ten years. If he disappeared after the age of seventy-five years, an absence of five years shall be sufficient in order that his succession may be opened. (n)
Art. 391, NCC. The following shall be presumed dead for all purposes, including the division of the estate among the heirs:
(1) A person on board a vessel lost during a sea voyage, or an aeroplane which is missing, who has not been heard of for four years since the loss of the vessel or aeroplane;
(2) A person in the armed forces who has taken part in war, and has been missing for four years;
(3) A person who has been in danger of death under other circumstances and his existence has not been known for four years. (n)
Art. 392, NCC. If the absentee appears, or without appearing his existence is proved, he shall recover his property in the condition in which it may be found, and the price of any property that may have been alienated or the property acquired therewith; but he cannot claim either fruits or rents. (194)
Art. 41, FC. A marriage contracted by any person during subsistence of a previous marriage shall be null and void, unless before the celebration of the subsequent marriage, the prior spouse had been absent for four consecutive years and the spouse present has a well-founded belief that the absent spouse was already dead. In case of disappearance where there is danger of death under the circumstances set forth in the provisions of Article 391 of the Civil Code, an absence of only two years shall be sufficient.
For the purpose of contracting the subsequent marriage under the preceding paragraph the spouse present must institute a summary proceeding as provided in this Code for the declaration of presumptive death of the absentee, without prejudice to the effect of reappearance of the absent spouse. (83a)
Samplex (Uribe quiz compilations): "X, who was not in danger of death, disappeared at the age of 76 … presumed dead for purposes of succession after an absence of: (a) 1 year (b) 5 years (c) 7 years (d) 10 years (e) 2 years" → (b) 5 years (disappeared after 75). "X, in danger of death, disappeared at the age of 76 … (f) 4 years from his disappearance" → (f): Art. 391 governs; age is irrelevant. "X, not in danger, disappeared at age 69" → (d) 10 years. T/F: "The absentee shall not be presumed dead for the purpose of opening his succession till after an absence of ten years" → keyed false: the statement omits the five-year rule for one who disappeared after 75 (and Art. 391 cases).
Tolentino (Arts. 390–391): the presumption of death arises by law, without any judicial declaration, and may be invoked in any action; there is no independent proceeding to declare a person presumptively dead, except for remarriage under FC 41. A judicial declaration adds nothing to the presumption, which stays prima facie and yields to proof that the absentee is alive (Szatraw; Lukban v. Republic). FC 41 amended Art. 390 (4 years for remarriage) and Art. 391 (2 years). Art. 391 covers "missing persons", whose disappearance points to a catastrophe: "vessel" includes all watercraft and "aeroplane" all aircraft; the vessel must be lost on a sea voyage (including river mouths and canals, but not inland waters). "War" covers all military operations, and the persons covered include nurses, doctors, guides, guerrillas and reporters with the armed forces, but only if they disappeared during military operations. "Other danger" includes earthquakes, fires, explosions, floods, mine cave-ins, volcanic eruptions and landslides; count from the last day of the danger, or, for expeditions, from when they should have ended.
Eastern Shipping Lines v. Lucero (124 SCRA 425, 1983)

Captain Julio Lucero's contract was to end on the arrival of the M/V Eastern Minicon in Manila. On the voyage from Hong Kong, the ship sent three messages reporting a leak and that the crew were abandoning ship; it was never heard from again. His wife refused death benefits and claimed the monthly allotments instead, arguing that under Art. 391(1) he could not be presumed dead until four years had passed, so his contract continued. Held: she is entitled to death benefits, not allotments. "There is thus enough evidence to show the circumstances attending the loss and disappearance of the M/V Eastern Minicon and its crew. The foregoing facts, quite logically, are sufficient to lead Us to a moral certainty that the vessel had sunk and that the persons aboard had perished with it. Upon this premise, the rule on presumption of death under Article 391(1) of the Civil Code must yield to the rule of preponderance of evidence. As this Court said in Joaquin vs. Navarro, 'Where there are facts, known or knowable, from which a rational conclusion can be made, the presumption does not step in, and the rule of preponderance of evidence controls.'"

Valmonte v. Villaroman (class reviewer)

Heirs of a long-absent owner, invoking the presumption of his death after seven years, sued to recover land held by others in open, continuous and adverse possession. Held: the action fails. "When a person's right to recover property had already prescribed during their lifetime due to another's open, continuous, and adverse possession for the statutory period, that right is extinguished and cannot be transmitted to heirs. Likewise, the presumption of death after seven years of absence does not revive or preserve any extinguished property right."

FC 41 vs. Arts. 390–391: for remarriage, the FC rules (4 or 2 years, a well-founded belief after diligent search, and a summary judicial declaration) displace the Civil Code periods; for all other purposes the presumption needs no declaration. The FC 41 judgment is immediately final and executory (FC 247); the State's remedy is certiorari (see Summary Proceedings). The subsequent marriage is terminated by the recording of an affidavit of reappearance (FC 42); see Void Marriages.
Tolentino (Art. 392): the reappearing absentee recovers his property as it is found, plus the price of what was sold or the property bought with it, but not the fruits or rents: those who held it in good faith keep them (compare Art. 396). His action is imprescriptible as to his ownership, subject to acquisitive prescription by others.

Effect of absence on the absentee's contingent rights (Arts. 393–396)

Art. 393, NCC. Whoever claims a right pertaining to a person whose existence is not recognized must prove that he was living at the time his existence was necessary in order to acquire said right. (195)
Art. 394, NCC. Without prejudice to the provision of the preceding article, upon the opening of a succession to which an absentee is called, his share shall accrue to his coheirs, unless he has heirs, assigns, or a representative. They shall all, as the case may be, make an inventory of the property. (196a)
Art. 395, NCC. The provisions of the preceding article are understood to be without prejudice to the action of petition for inheritance or other rights which are vested in the absentee, his representatives or successors in interest. These rights shall not be extinguished save by lapse of time fixed for prescription. In the record that is made in the Registry of the real estate which accrues to the coheirs, the circumstance of its being subject to the provisions of this article shall be stated. (197)
Art. 396, NCC. Those who may have entered upon the inheritance shall appropriate the fruits received in good faith so long as the absentee does not appear, or while his representatives or successors in interest do not bring the proper actions. (198)

Tolentino's illustration (Art. 393): X disappeared in 1940. Y died in 1942, leaving a will instituting X as heir. In 1950, X's children, invoking the presumption that X is dead, want to take the inheritance Y left to X. May they?

Only if they prove that X was alive when Y died in 1942. The presumption of death does not help them, and a dead person cannot succeed by will; whoever claims a right of an absentee must prove he was living when his existence was necessary to acquire it (Art. 393). Otherwise the share accrues to X's co-heirs under Art. 394 (or, X being only an instituted heir, follows the rules on accretion and intestacy), subject to an action for petition of inheritance if X turns out to have been alive (Art. 395).

Quiz traps (absence): "A friend of the absentee may ask for the declaration of absence" → false (Art. 385; a friend may ask only for a provisional representative under Art. 381). "The declaration of absence takes effect upon its publication" → false: six months after publication (Art. 386). "If the absentee left an administrator, his absence may be declared after two years" → false: five years (Art. 384). "After seven years, the absentee is presumed dead for all purposes" → false: except succession (Art. 390). "A person lost at sea is presumed dead for all purposes except succession after four years" → false: including the division of the estate (Art. 391). "The reappearing absentee recovers his property with its fruits" → false (Art. 392). "A wife-administratrix may sell the absent husband's property" → false without judicial authority (Art. 388).
