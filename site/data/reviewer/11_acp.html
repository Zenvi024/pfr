Absolute Community of Property

Title IV, Chapter 3, Family Code, Arts. 88–104 (syllabus X.D). Covers commencement (Art. 88), waiver (Art. 89), suppletory co-ownership rules (Art. 90), what the community consists of and what it excludes (Arts. 91–92), the presumption (Art. 93), charges and obligations (Arts. 94–95), administration and disposition (Arts. 96–98), dissolution (Art. 99), separation in fact and abandonment (Arts. 100–101), liquidation (Arts. 102–103), and two or more marriages (Art. 104). Cases: Ko v. Aramburo, Abrenica v. Law Firm of Abrenica, Delizo v. Delizo.

The two-step method Uribe wants on every property problem

  1. Find the regime. Is there a valid marriage settlement (in writing, signed, executed before the wedding, with the Art. 78–79 parties)? If yes, follow it. If not, when was the marriage celebrated? On or after August 3, 1988 → ACP (Art. 75). Before → CPG (NCC 119). Remarried without liquidating a prior regime → complete separation (Arts. 103, 130). Void marriage → Art. 147 or 148 co-ownership, not ACP or CPG.
  2. Classify the property within that regime. Owned at the celebration or acquired after? Gratuitous or onerous? Fruits? Exclusive use? Jewelry? Does the spouse have legitimate descendants by a former marriage?

Arts. 88–90: commencement, waiver and suppletory rules

Art. 88, FC. The absolute community of property between spouses shall commence at the precise moment that the marriage is celebrated. Any stipulation, express or implied, for the commencement of the community regime at any other time shall be void. (145a)
  • "Precise moment": property owned by a spouse at 10:00 a.m. becomes community property at 5:00 p.m., when the marriage is celebrated.
  • Any stipulation that the regime starts earlier (e.g., on the date of the settlement) or later (e.g., one year after the wedding) is void. The rest of the settlement survives.

CivRev midterms MCQ #25 (Uribe): In the will of X, a parcel of land was validly bequeathed to A, who was married to B at 5 PM on June 1, 2019. A and B orally agreed on a conjugal partnership of gains. At 10 AM on June 1, 2019, X died. The land will form part of the: (a) conjugal partnership property; (b) community property; (c) separate property of A; (d) estate of X; (e) property of the State.

B (community property). Successional rights are transmitted at death (Art. 777, NCC), so A owned the land at 10 AM. The oral settlement is void (Art. 77), so the regime is ACP (Art. 75). The ACP commenced at 5 PM (Art. 88) and consists of all property owned by the spouses at the time of the celebration (Art. 91). The land is not excluded under Art. 92(1), because it was acquired before the marriage, not during it. Sir: "Even before the solemnization, A being the owner, under the FC in an ACP regime, properties owned before become community property."

Compare situation 9 (CivRev 2025): same facts, but the spouses executed a valid settlement adopting CPG. Nature of the land?

Exclusive property of A, because it was brought to the marriage as his own (Art. 109(1)). The difference is entirely in the regime. And the 2018 situationer: a CPG settlement stipulated that the regime would commence on the date of the settlement, 15 days before the wedding. That stipulation is void (Arts. 88, 107). The CPG begins only at the precise moment of the celebration, so the land inherited at 9 AM that day remains A's exclusive property "entirely".

Art. 89, FC. No waiver of rights, shares and effects of the absolute community of property during the marriage can be made except in case of judicial separation of property.
When the waiver takes place upon a judicial separation of property, or after the marriage has been dissolved or annulled, the same shall appear in a public instrument and shall be recorded as provided in Article 77. The creditors of the spouse who made such waiver may petition the court to rescind the waiver to the extent of the amount sufficient to cover the amount of their credits. (146a)
  • During the marriage: no waiver, except in a judicial separation of property.
  • Upon judicial separation, or after dissolution or annulment: the waiver must be in a public instrument and recorded as in Art. 77 (LCR and registries of property).
  • The waiving spouse's creditors may ask the court to rescind the waiver to the extent of their credits (an accion pauliana).
  • A valid waiver affects the division of net assets (Art. 102(4): "unless there has been a voluntary waiver of such share provided in this Code").
Quiz trap: contrast with the marriage settlement itself. A settlement needs only a writing (Art. 77). A waiver of the ACP share needs a public instrument (Art. 89). Also, "no waiver … during the marriage can be made" is true only with the exception: "except in case of judicial separation of property."
Art. 90, FC. The provisions on co-ownership shall apply to the absolute community of property between the spouses in all matters not provided for in this Chapter. (n)
Quiz trap (pairs with Art. 108): ACP → suppletory rules on co-ownership (Art. 90). CPG → suppletory rules on the contract of partnership (Art. 108). His TF item "The conjugal partnership shall be governed by the rules of co-ownership in all that is not in conflict …" is False.

Art. 91: what the community consists of

Art. 91, FC. Unless otherwise provided in this Chapter or in the marriage settlements, the community property shall consist of all the property owned by the spouses at the time of the celebration of the marriage or acquired thereafter. (197a)

The general rule covers everything: property owned at the moment of the wedding, however acquired (onerous or gratuitous, inherited or bought), plus everything acquired afterward, including winnings from gambling (Art. 95), hidden treasure, and salaries. Exceptions: Art. 92 ("otherwise provided in this Chapter") and the marriage settlement.

Quiz trap (his most repeated ACP item; Quiz 4 #8; Quiz #3 pair 5; Quiz comp TF9; Quiz 3 key #13): "The community property shall consist of all the property owned by the spouses at the time of the celebration of the marriage or acquired thereafter." False: it omits "Unless otherwise provided in this Chapter or in the marriage settlements". Some property owned at the celebration (Art. 92(3)) and some acquired after (Art. 92(1)–(2)) are excluded.

B practically holds ⅝ of the land.

Art. 92: what is excluded from the community

Art. 92, FC. The following shall be excluded from the community property:
(1) Property acquired during the marriage by gratuitous title by either spouse, and the fruits as well as the income thereof, if any, unless it is expressly provided by the donor, testator or grantor that they shall form part of the community property;
(2) Property for personal and exclusive use of either spouse. However, jewelry shall form part of the community property;
(3) Property acquired before the marriage by either spouse who has legitimate descendants by a former marriage, and the fruits as well as the income, if any, of such property. (201a)

Art. 92(1): gratuitous title during the marriage

  • The exclusion is limited to acquisitions during the marriage. Property inherited or donated before the wedding is community property under Art. 91 (unless Art. 92(3) applies).
  • The donor or testator can override the exclusion by expressly providing that the property shall form part of the community. A statement such as "inherited by A" means A alone inherited.
  • When both spouses inherit (e.g., from their own child), each spouse's share is that spouse's separate property. Sir (2018): "Is it possible that this is not conjugal or community property pero silang dalawa ang magmamana? Yes, if the one who died is their child."

Art. 92(2): personal and exclusive use, but jewelry is community

Quiz trap (CivRev 2025 Quiz 4 #12; midterms #34 and #48; Quiz comp #18; Quiz #3 MCQ 10): "Jewelry for personal and exclusive use" is not excluded, so an option that names it is wrong. The law's own exception is that jewelry "shall form part of the community property" even when it is for one spouse's personal and exclusive use.

Art. 92(3): property of a spouse with legitimate descendants by a former marriage

  • Three elements: (a) property acquired before the marriage; (b) the spouse has legitimate descendants; (c) by a former marriage. The fruits and income of that property are also excluded.
  • Rationale: to protect the legitimes and inheritance of the children of the first marriage, so that their parent's property does not pass into a new community with a stranger. Sir (situation 5): "If the spouse has legitimate descendants by a prior marriage … they are separate properties of the spouse, kasi para sa legitimate descendants niya yon."
  • Illegitimate children, or legitimate descendants not "by a former marriage", do not trigger the exclusion.
Spouses Abrenica v. Law Firm of Abrenica, Tungol and Tibayan (G.R. No. 180572, June 18, 2012)

In execution proceedings against Atty. Abrenica, his second wife, Joena, claimed that two motor vehicles (bought in 1992 and 1997) and a house and lot were community property of their marriage and could not be levied for his obligation. Held: Art. 92(3) excludes from the community property acquired before the marriage by a spouse who has legitimate descendants by a former marriage, together with its fruits and income. "Neither these two vehicles nor the house and lot belong to the second marriage." The properties were therefore not community property of Joena's marriage and could answer for Atty. Abrenica's liability.

Art. 93: the presumption of community

Art. 93, FC. Property acquired during the marriage is presumed to belong to the community, unless it is proved that it is one of those excluded therefrom. (160)
  • Trigger: acquisition during the marriage. The presumption does not depend on the title (onerous or gratuitous) or on whose name the property is registered in. The burden is on the party claiming exclusive ownership.
  • Condition sine qua non: the party invoking the presumption must first prove that the property was acquired during the marriage (Dela Peña v. Avila; Andrade v. Tan; Go v. Yamane; these CPG cases on NCC 160 and FC 116 apply equally to Art. 93).
  • The presumption is disputable. It yields to proof that the property falls under Art. 92 or the settlement.
Quiz trap (CivRev 2025 Quiz 4 #20; Quiz #3 MCQ 6): "A parcel of land is presumed to belong to the community property if: (a) acquired by gratuitous title; (b) acquired by onerous title; (c) acquired before the marriage; (d) registered during the marriage; (e) none of the above." E, because the correct trigger, "acquired during the marriage", is not among the options. Registration is not acquisition: "malay mo it was registered during the marriage" but acquired before. (In the older version, where "acquired during the marriage" was an option, that option is the answer.)
Ko v. Aramburo (G.R. No. 190995, August 9, 2017)

Listed in the syllabus under "Presumption of ACP, FC 93 cf. NCC 160". The dispute was between the heirs of two siblings over seven parcels in Tabaco City, Albay, all titled in the name of Corazon. The plaintiffs alleged that Virginia and her husband Simeon, together with Corazon and her husband Felix, acquired the properties from the Spouses Casaul through a Deed of Cession dated April 10, 1970. Corazon countered that the properties were her parents', that Simeon sold his entire one-half share to her on December 14, 1974, and that the action had prescribed. The syllabus places the case under the presumption that property acquired during the marriage belongs to the community or partnership, and that the presumption must be overcome by proof. Verify the holding against the full text before relying on details: the case was not digested in your materials, and the case sites could not be reached to confirm the Court's reasoning.

Under the Civil Code (NCC 160): "All property of the marriage is presumed to belong to the conjugal partnership, unless it be proved that it pertains exclusively to the husband or to the wife." The cases in the CPG section apply here too: the presumption needs proof of acquisition during the marriage (Torela, Dela Peña, Andrade); it does not apply when there is no showing of when the property was acquired (Maramba, Magallon, Cuenca); and "married to" in a title is merely descriptive (Magallon, Go-Bangayan).

Arts. 94–95: charges upon and obligations of the community

Art. 94, FC. The absolute community of property shall be liable for:
(1) The support of the spouses, their common children, and legitimate children of either spouse; however, the support of illegitimate children shall be governed by the provisions of this Code on Support;
(2) All debts and obligations contracted during the marriage by the designated administrator-spouse for the benefit of the community, or by both spouses, or by one spouse with the consent of the other;
(3) Debts and obligations contracted by either spouse without the consent of the other to the extent that the family may have been benefited;
(4) All taxes, liens, charges and expenses, including major or minor repairs, upon the community property;
(5) All taxes and expenses for mere preservation made during marriage upon the separate property of either spouse used by the family;
(6) Expenses to enable either spouse to commence or complete a professional or vocational course, or other activity for self-improvement;
(7) Ante-nuptial debts of either spouse insofar as they have redounded to the benefit of the family;
(8) The value of what is donated or promised by both spouses in favor of their common legitimate children for the exclusive purpose of commencing or completing a professional or vocational course or other activity for self-improvement;
(9) Ante-nuptial debts of either spouse other than those falling under paragraph (7) of this Article, the support of illegitimate children of either spouse, and liabilities incurred by either spouse by reason of a crime or a quasi-delict, in case of absence or insufficiency of the exclusive property of the debtor-spouse, the payment of which shall be considered as advances to be deducted from the share of the debtor-spouse upon liquidation of the community; and
(10) Expenses of litigation between the spouses unless the suit is found to be groundless.
If the community property is insufficient to cover the foregoing liabilities, except those falling under paragraph (9), the spouses shall be solidarily liable for the unpaid balance with their separate properties. (161a, 162a, 163a, 202a-205a)

Breakdown: four groups

  • Family needs: (1) support; (6) self-improvement of a spouse; (8) donations by both spouses to common legitimate children for education.
  • Debts: (2) debts with consent or by the administrator for the benefit of the community (no benefit test if both consented); (3) debts without consent, to the extent the family benefited; (7) ante-nuptial debts that redounded to the family.
  • Property: (4) taxes, liens, charges and repairs (major or minor) upon community property; (5) taxes and expenses for mere preservation of separate property used by the family.
  • Advances (par. 9): personal ante-nuptial debts, support of a spouse's illegitimate children, and crime or quasi-delict liabilities. These are paid by the community only if the debtor-spouse's exclusive property is absent or insufficient, and only as advances deducted from that spouse's share on liquidation.
  • Last paragraph: if the community is insufficient, the spouses are solidarily liable with their separate properties, except for par. (9) items.
Quiz trap (CivRev midterms pair #3, Statement 2): "The absolute community of property shall be liable for all expenses for mere preservation made during marriage upon the separate property of either spouse." That is not required. Who cares kung may benefit o wala. Ang term is 'used'. Basta ginagamit." The same student confused (5) with (7): "these are for preservation, not ante-nuptial debts."
Uribe (situation 10, CivRev 2025; 2018 situationer): before the marriage, A inherited a building. After the wedding, ₱5M was spent to retrofit it (or ₱2M for preservation). Chargeable to whom? It depends on the regime. ACP: the building is community property unless Art. 92(3) applies. If it is A's separate property, the expense is chargeable to the community only if the building is used by the family (Art. 94(5)); otherwise it is A's. CPG: the building is A's exclusive property (brought to the marriage), and preservation expenses are chargeable to the partnership whether or not used by the family (Art. 121(5) has no such qualifier). Why the difference? In the CPG, the fruits of the exclusive property go to the partnership (Art. 117(3)), so the partnership bears the preservation costs. In the ACP, the fruits of separate property are excluded (Art. 92(1), (3)), so the community pays only when the family uses the property.
Uribe (2018 situationer, Art. 94(9)): "Is there any other expense chargeable to ACP but not to CPG?" The support of a spouse's illegitimate children. In an ACP, whatever a spouse owns usually becomes community property, so the spouse with illegitimate children often has no separate property from which to support them. Art. 94(9) lets the community advance the support, deducted from that spouse's share upon liquidation. (In a CPG, the spouse keeps exclusive property for this purpose, and Art. 122 lets the partnership answer only after the Art. 121 responsibilities are covered.)

UP BOC 2007 TF: "An individual, while single, purchases a house and lot in 1990, and borrows money in 1992 to repair it. In 1995, such individual gets married while the debt is still being paid. After the marriage, the debt is still the responsibility of such individual."

False. The ACP is liable for ante-nuptial debts "insofar as they have redounded to the benefit of the family" (Art. 94(7)). Alternatively, the house and lot itself became community property in 1995 (Art. 91), and the debt for its repair is a charge on community property (Art. 94(4)).

Art. 95, FC. Whatever may be lost during the marriage in any game of chance, betting, sweepstakes, or any other kind of gambling, whether permitted or prohibited by law, shall be borne by the loser and shall not be charged to the community but any winnings therefrom shall form part of the community property. (164a)
Quiz trap: losses → borne by the loser, whether the gambling is legal or illegal; winnings → community. In the CPG, Art. 117(7) puts winnings in the partnership ("acquired by chance"), and Art. 123 has the same rule on losses.

Arts. 96–98: administration, disposition, wills and donations

Art. 96, FC. The administration and enjoyment of the community property shall belong to both spouses jointly. In case of disagreement, the husband's decision shall prevail, subject to recourse to the court by the wife for proper remedy, which must be availed of within five years from the date of the contract implementing such decision.
In the event that one spouse is incapacitated or otherwise unable to participate in the administration of the common properties, the other spouse may assume sole powers of administration. These powers do not include disposition or encumbrance without authority of the court or the written consent of the other spouse. In the absence of such authority or consent, the disposition or encumbrance shall be void. However, the transaction shall be construed as a continuing offer on the part of the consenting spouse and the third person, and may be perfected as a binding contract upon the acceptance by the other spouse or authorization by the court before the offer is withdrawn by either or both offerors. (206a)

Breakdown of Art. 96

  1. Joint administration and enjoyment.
  2. Disagreement: the husband's decision prevails, but the wife may go to court within five years from the date of the contract implementing the decision.
  3. Sole administration by one spouse if the other is incapacitated or otherwise unable to participate. Sole administration does not include disposition or encumbrance.
  4. Disposition or encumbrance needs the court's authority or the other spouse's written consent. Without either, it is void, but it is construed as a continuing offer by the consenting spouse and the third person, perfected upon the other spouse's acceptance or the court's authorization before the offer is withdrawn.
The "husband's decision prevails" clause and equal protection. Art. 96 still reads this way and is quoted verbatim on quizzes. Commentators note that it is in tension with the equality clause (Art. II, Sec. 14; Art. XIII, Sec. 14, 1987 Constitution) and with RA 9710 (Magna Carta of Women), which mandates review of discriminatory provisions. For quiz items, answer according to the text of Art. 96.

2017 / 2015 Bar (Danny–Elsa / Marco–Gina): The spouses married in 2002 (1989), without a settlement. Years later the wife left the conjugal home and the children to live with another man. The husband, needing money for the children's tuition, sold a parcel of land registered in his name, which he had bought before the marriage, without her consent. Valid, void or voidable?

Void. The regime is ACP (Art. 75), so the land became community property when the marriage was celebrated even though it is registered in his name alone (Art. 91), and no Art. 92 exclusion applies. Disposition of community property needs the other spouse's written consent or court authority (Art. 96). Her abandonment does not dispense with it. Under Art. 100(2), in a separation in fact, the required consent is replaced by judicial authorization in a summary proceeding, which he did not obtain. Good motive (tuition) does not cure the defect.

2025 Bar: A and B married in 1991 without a prenuptial agreement. In 1997, A forged B's signature on a promissory note and on a real estate mortgage over three parcels acquired during the marriage. PCB foreclosed and bought the lands. B sued to nullify the mortgage and the certificate of sale. Is B correct?

Yes. The parcels are presumed community property (Arts. 75, 93). An encumbrance without the other spouse's consent is void under Art. 96. It remains only a continuing offer that B never accepted. The foreclosure and sale built on the void mortgage are likewise void (UPLC suggested answer, citing PNB v. Reyes, Jr., G.R. No. 212483, October 5, 2016).

UP BOC 2011 MCQ: The husband assumed sole administration of the family's mango plantation since his wife worked abroad. Without her knowledge, he entered into an antichretic transaction giving a company possession of the plantation with power to harvest and sell the fruits to pay his loan. Standing of the contract?

D: a continuing offer, perfected only upon the wife's acceptance or the court's authorization (Art. 96 2nd par.; Art. 124 2nd par. has the same text). Antichresis is an encumbrance, which a sole administrator cannot make alone.

Art. 97, FC. Either spouse may dispose by will of his or her interest in the community property. (n)
Art. 98, FC. Neither spouse may donate any community property without the consent of the other. However, either spouse may, without the consent of the other, make moderate donations from the community property for charity or on occasions of family rejoicing or family distress. (n)
  • Art. 97: a will takes effect only at death, which dissolves the community, so a spouse may bequeath his or her interest (his or her eventual share in the net assets). The spouse cannot will specific community property as if it were wholly his or her own.
  • Art. 98: donation of community property requires the other spouse's consent, except moderate donations for charity or on occasions of family rejoicing or family distress. Compare Art. 87 (between spouses: family rejoicing only).

Arts. 100–101: separation in fact and abandonment

Art. 100, FC. The separation in fact between husband and wife shall not affect the regime of absolute community except that:
(1) The spouse who leaves the conjugal home or refuses to live therein, without just cause, shall not have the right to be supported;
(2) When the consent of one spouse to any transaction of the other is required by law, judicial authorization shall be obtained in a summary proceeding;
(3) In the absence of sufficient community property, the separate property of both spouses shall be solidarily liable for the support of the family. The spouse present shall, upon proper petition in a summary proceeding, be given judicial authority to administer or encumber any specific separate property of the other spouse and use the fruits or proceeds thereof to satisfy the latter's share. (178a)
Art. 101, FC. If a spouse without just cause abandons the other or fails to comply with his or her obligations to the family, the aggrieved spouse may petition the court for receivership, for judicial separation of property or for authority to be the sole administrator of the absolute community, subject to such precautionary conditions as the court may impose.
The obligations to the family mentioned in the preceding paragraph refer to marital, parental or property relations.
A spouse is deemed to have abandoned the other when her or she has left the conjugal dwelling without intention of returning. The spouse who has left the conjugal dwelling for a period of three months or has failed within the same period to give any information as to his or her whereabouts shall be prima facie presumed to have no intention of returning to the conjugal dwelling. (178a)

(3) Separation in fact does not dispense with the other spouse's consent. It replaces the consent with judicial authorization in a summary proceeding.

Art. 99: when the absolute community terminates

Art. 99, FC. The absolute community terminates:
(1) Upon the death of either spouse;
(2) When there is a decree of legal separation;
(3) When the marriage is annulled or declared void; or
(4) In case of judicial separation of property during the marriage under Articles 134 to 138. (175a)
Quiz traps: "The absolute community terminates when there is a decree of legal separation" is True (TF compilation #4), and so is "The conjugal partnership terminates when there is a decree of legal separation" (Quiz 3 key #16; Art. 126(2)). Legal separation dissolves the regime but not the marriage. The old MCQ "The following will dissolve both the absolute community and the marriage, except:" turns on this: only death, annulment and nullity end the marriage.
Uribe (2018 situationer): "The declaration of nullity, annulment, legal separation: there will be a dissolution of property relations. Do you agree?" No. With annulment and legal separation, yes. When the marriage is void, there was never an ACP or CPG to dissolve. The property relations were governed all along by Art. 147 or 148 co-ownership. "Kapag void, co-ownership ang magogovern. There is nothing to dissolve." (Art. 99(3) speaks of "declared void" because, in practice, a void marriage's property relations are settled in the same proceeding; Arts. 50 and 43(2) apply to Art. 40 and Art. 45 cases.)

Art. 102: liquidation after dissolution (other than by death)

Art. 102, FC. Upon dissolution of the absolute community regime, the following procedure shall apply:
(1) An inventory shall be prepared, listing separately all the properties of the absolute community and the exclusive properties of each spouse.
(2) The debts and obligations of the absolute community shall be paid out of its assets. In case of insufficiency of said assets, the spouses shall be solidarily liable for the unpaid balance with their separate properties in accordance with the provisions of the second paragraph of Article 94.
(3) Whatever remains of the exclusive properties of the spouses shall thereafter be delivered to each of them.
(4) The net remainder of the properties of the absolute community shall constitute its net assets, which shall be divided equally between husband and wife, unless a different proportion or division was agreed upon in the marriage settlements, or unless there has been a voluntary waiver of such share provided in this Code. For purpose of computing the net profits subject to forfeiture in accordance with Articles 43, No. (2) and 63, No. (2), the said profits shall be the increase in value between the market value of the community property at the time of the celebration of the marriage and the market value at the time of its dissolution.
(5) The presumptive legitimes of the common children shall be delivered upon partition, in accordance with Article 51.
(6) Unless otherwise agreed upon by the parties, in the partition of the properties, the conjugal dwelling and the lot on which it is situated shall be adjudicated to the spouse with whom the majority of the common children choose to remain. Children below the age of seven years are deemed to have chosen the mother, unless the court has decided otherwise. In case there in no such majority, the court shall decide, taking into consideration the best interests of said children. (n)

Steps in order

  1. Inventory: community property and each spouse's exclusive property, listed separately.
  2. Pay community debts from community assets; if insufficient, the spouses are solidarily liable with their separate property (Art. 94, 2nd par.).
  3. Deliver the remaining exclusive properties to each spouse.
  4. Divide the net assets equally, unless the settlement provides another proportion or a spouse validly waived his or her share. For forfeiture (Arts. 43(2), 63(2)), "net profits" = market value of community property at dissolution minus market value at the celebration.
  5. Deliver the presumptive legitimes of the common children (Art. 51).
  6. Conjugal dwelling and lot → to the spouse with whom the majority of the common children choose to remain; children below seven are deemed to have chosen the mother unless the court decides otherwise; no majority → the court decides on the children's best interests.
Quiz trap (forfeiture): in an ACP, the forfeitable "net profits" are not the whole share. They are only the increase in market value of the community property between the celebration and the dissolution (Art. 102(4)). In legal separation, the guilty spouse's share in the net profits is forfeited in favor of the common children, or, if none, the children of the guilty spouse by a previous marriage, or, in default, the innocent spouse (Art. 63(2) in relation to Art. 43(2)).

Art. 103: liquidation upon death, and the mandatory separation rule

Art. 103, FC. Upon the termination of the marriage by death, the community property shall be liquidated in the same proceeding for the settlement of the estate of the deceased.
If no judicial settlement proceeding is instituted, the surviving spouse shall liquidate the community property either judicially or extra-judicially within one year from the death of the deceased spouse. If upon the lapse of the said period, no liquidation is made, any disposition or encumbrance involving the community property of the terminated marriage shall be void.
Should the surviving spouse contract a subsequent marriage without compliance with the foregoing requirements, a mandatory regime of complete separation of property shall govern the property relations of the subsequent marriage. (n)
  • Liquidate in the estate proceedings. If none, the surviving spouse liquidates judicially or extra-judicially within one year from the death.
  • After the period lapses without liquidation, any disposition or encumbrance of community property of the terminated marriage is void.
  • If the survivor remarries without liquidating, the subsequent marriage is governed by a mandatory regime of complete separation of property, whatever the new settlement says.
  • Rationale (Quiz 4 #13 discussion): liquidation is needed to deliver the legitimes of the heirs and to pay the debts of the deceased. Mandatory separation protects the heirs of the first marriage from having that property mixed into a new regime.
Quiz trap (ACP vs CPG period): Art. 103 (ACP) gives one year. Art. 130 (CPG) gives six months ("within six months from the death … If upon the lapse of the six-month period …"). The rest of the two articles is parallel. Expect a swapped period.
Compare Art. 40 and Art. 53. Art. 53 makes a subsequent marriage void if the former spouses failed to comply with Art. 52 (recording of the judgment of annulment or nullity, the partition and distribution of the properties, and the delivery of the children's presumptive legitimes). Art. 103 deals with a marriage terminated by death: the subsequent marriage is valid, but it falls under mandatory separation of property. Do not mix them up.

Art. 104: two or more marriages liquidated together; Delizo v. Delizo

Art. 104, FC. Whenever the liquidation of the community properties of two or more marriages contracted by the same person before the effectivity of this Code is carried out simultaneously, the respective capital, fruits and income of each community shall be determined upon such proof as may be considered according to the rules of evidence. In case of doubt as to which community the existing properties belong, the same shall be divided between the different communities in proportion to the capital and duration of each. (189a)
Delizo v. Delizo (69 SCRA 216, 1976)

Nicolas Delizo married twice. His first conjugal partnership (with Rosa Villasfer, three children) and his second (with Dorotea de Ocampo, nine children) were liquidated together after his death, and the evidence did not clearly show which partnership acquired which properties. Applying NCC 189 (the source of FC 104), the Court divided the total mass between the two partnerships in proportion to the duration of each: 18/64 to the first and 46/64 to the second. Each partnership's net remainder was then divided equally between the spouses (NCC 185). Nicolas's half of both partnerships formed his estate, distributed equally among his twelve children, with his widow taking a share equal to one child's. In the digest's computation, Rosa's share was 9/64 and Dorotea's 23/64, plus Dorotea's share as heir. Doctrine: in case of doubt about which of several communities or partnerships owns existing properties, divide them in proportion to the capital and the duration of each (FC 104 adds capital to the NCC's duration).

Checklist: ACP in one page

  1. Commences at the precise moment of the celebration; any stipulation otherwise is void (Art. 88).
  2. No waiver during the marriage, except upon judicial separation; a waiver then or after dissolution must be in a public instrument, recorded, and is rescissible by creditors (Art. 89).
  3. Suppletory: co-ownership (Art. 90).
  4. Consists of all property owned at the celebration or acquired after, unless otherwise provided by the Chapter or the settlement (Art. 91).
  5. Excluded: (1) gratuitous acquisitions during the marriage, with their fruits, unless the giver says otherwise; (2) personal and exclusive use, except jewelry; (3) property before the marriage of a spouse with legitimate descendants by a former marriage, with its fruits (Art. 92).
  6. Presumption: acquired during the marriage → community, unless proved excluded (Art. 93).
  7. Charges (Art. 94): note (5) "used by the family", (3) and (7) "benefit / redounded", (9) advances, and solidary liability except (9).
  8. Gambling: losses → loser; winnings → community (Art. 95).
  9. Joint administration; disagreement → husband prevails, wife's recourse within five years from the contract; disposition or encumbrance without written consent or court authority → void but a continuing offer (Art. 96).
  10. Either spouse may will his or her interest (Art. 97); no donation of community property without consent, except moderate donations for charity, family rejoicing or family distress (Art. 98).
  11. Terminates: death, legal separation decree, annulment or nullity, judicial separation of property (Art. 99).
  12. Separation in fact: regime unaffected except no support for the spouse who left without just cause, judicial authorization in place of consent, and solidary liability of separate property for support (Art. 100). Abandonment: receivership, judicial separation, or sole administration; three months → prima facie presumption (Art. 101).
  13. Liquidation (Art. 102): inventory → debts → exclusive property returned → net assets equally (forfeitable profits = increase in market value) → presumptive legitimes → conjugal dwelling to the majority's choice (below 7 → mother).
  14. Death: liquidate in the estate proceeding or within one year; otherwise dispositions are void; remarriage without liquidation → mandatory complete separation (Art. 103). CPG: six months (Art. 130).
