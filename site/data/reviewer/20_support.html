Support

Title VIII, Family Code, Arts. 194–208 (syllabus XV). What support covers, who must give it and in what order, which property answers for it, how much and when it is paid, support pendente lite, and how the right ends. Cases: Pelayo v. Lauron, Sanchez v. Zulueta, Reyes v. Ines-Luciano, Lerma v. CA, Abella v. Cabañero, Melgar v. People, Canonizado v. Benitez; from the class reviewer, Mangonon, Lim v. Lim, De Asis, Perkins, Altobano-Ruiz, Otamias.

What support comprises (Art. 194)

Art. 194, FC. Support comprises everything indispensable for sustenance, dwelling, clothing, medical attendance, education and transportation, in keeping with the financial capacity of the family.
The education of the person entitled to be supported referred to in the preceding paragraph shall include his schooling or training for some profession, trade or vocation, even beyond the age of majority. Transportation shall include expenses in going to and from school, or to and from place of work. (290a)
Tolentino (Art. 194): natural support is what is absolutely indispensable for subsistence; civil support is what the recipient needs to live according to his social position. The Civil Code kept the distinction; the Family Code erased it. Support does not include a simple loan of money to the wife (Perkins v. Perkins, 57 Phil. 223). The obligation has a permanent aspect (physical needs, for the recipient's lifetime unless it ceases) and a temporary aspect (intellectual development, only while acquiring a profession, trade or vocation). If the recipient began a course while still a minor, the obligor must keep paying until it is finished, even after the recipient comes of age.
Quiz trap: "Education as part of support ends when the child reaches majority" → false: it includes schooling or training "even beyond the age of majority". 2010 Bar: children aged 18 and 19 who squandered a ₱3 million education fund may still ask for support for their schooling; squandering the fund does not extinguish the parents' obligation.

Who must support whom (Arts. 195–196)

Art. 195, FC. Subject to the provisions of the succeeding articles, the following are obliged to support each other to the whole extent set forth in the preceding article:
(1) The spouses;
(2) Legitimate ascendants and descendants;
(3) Parents and their legitimate children and the legitimate and illegitimate children of the latter;
(4) Parents and their illegitimate children and the legitimate and illegitimate children of the latter; and
(5) Legitimate brothers and sisters, whether of full or half-blood. (291a)
Art. 196, FC. Brothers and sisters not legitimately related, whether of the full or half-blood, are likewise bound to support each other to the full extent set forth in Article 194, except only when the need for support of the brother or sister, being of age, is due to a cause imputable to the claimant's fault or negligence. (291a)
Uribe (CivRev Quiz 2, item 5; also Samplex T/F 9 and the PFR Quiz 3 key): "Brothers and sisters not legitimately related, whether of the full or half-blood, are likewise bound to support each other." False, because Art. 196 has an exception (claimant of age whose need is due to his own fault or negligence). Sir's warning: do not attack the premise ("brothers and sisters not legitimately related, whether of the full or half-blood" is correct as written); what is wrong is the unqualified conclusion. "Huwag niyo palitan ang premise or rule unless it is misleading." And: "Do not say 'should,' unless it should be tomorrow." Asked whether Art. 196's exception applies to ascendants and descendants, the answer was no: it applies only to siblings not legitimately related.
Tolentino (Arts. 195–196): the FC corrected an inequality in the Civil Code (Art. 291), under which illegitimate descendants of legitimate children could not claim support from their grandparents; now descendants, legitimate or illegitimate, of children legitimate or illegitimate, are on equal footing. Under the Civil Code, siblings were entitled only to natural support; the FC gives them civil support "to the full extent set forth in Article 194". Art. 196 is ambiguous: does the exception deny all support, or only the "full extent"? Tolentino: the intention is the same as under the old Code; the right to support ceases. Between spouses, the right depends on the wife's status as such: once the marriage is annulled she is not entitled to support, even pending the liquidation (Mendoza v. Parungao, 49 Phil. 271). A wife forced to sue for support is entitled to the expenses of litigation, including attorney's fees, as part of support; the same holds where the wife is rich and the husband poor. A mother who gave away her two-year-old child and never cared for her cannot later claim support from the child.
Pelayo v. Lauron (12 Phil. 453, 1909)

A physician was called one night by the defendants, husband and wife, to attend their daughter-in-law, who was giving birth in their house. She lived with her husband (the defendants' son) independently of them and was only accidentally in their house that night. The physician charged ₱500 and sued the parents-in-law, who had called him. Held: they are not liable. "The rendering of medical assistance in case of illness is comprised among the mutual obligations to which the spouses are bound by way of mutual support." The person bound to pay is the husband of the patient, not her father- and mother-in-law, who are strangers to that obligation; it is immaterial that he was not the one who called the doctor.

De Asis v. CA (G.R. No. 127578, February 15, 1999; class reviewer)

The mother of a minor sued the alleged father for support; she later moved to dismiss, manifesting that the defendant denied paternity, and the case was dismissed with prejudice. A second action for support was filed in the child's name. Held: not barred. "In order to claim support, filiation and/or paternity must first be shown between the claimant and the parent. However, paternity and filiation or the lack of the same is a relationship that must be judicially established and it is for the court to declare its existence or absence. It cannot be left to the will or agreement of the parties." The earlier dismissal amounted to a renunciation of future support, which is void (NCC 2035).

Abella v. Cabañero (G.R. No. 206647, August 9, 2017)

Not in your sources in full; from general recollection (verify). Richelle Abella sued Policarpio Cabañero for support of her minor daughter, alleging that he had sexually abused her while she was a minor and was the child's father. The lower courts dismissed the complaint for failure to state a cause of action, reasoning that the child's filiation had not first been established in a separate action. Held: reversed. Filiation may be proved, and support adjudged, in the same action; an illegitimate child need not file a separate action for recognition before suing for support. The case was remanded for trial on filiation and support.

Melgar v. People (G.R. No. 223477, February 14, 2018)

Not in your sources in full; from general recollection (verify). Melgar was charged under R.A. No. 9262, Sec. 5(i), for psychological violence through denial of financial support to his illegitimate son with AAA. He entered into a compromise on the civil aspect. Held: the compromise does not extinguish criminal liability. Deprivation or denial of financial support to the child is itself a form of economic abuse punished by Sec. 5(e); since the mental or emotional anguish required by Sec. 5(i) was not proved, but the denial of support was, he was convicted under Sec. 5(e) under the variance doctrine, the offense charged including the offense proved.

Altobano-Ruiz v. Atty. Ruiz (class reviewer)

A wife sought the disbarment of her husband, a lawyer convicted under R.A. 9262 for, among others, depriving her and their child of support, who even tried to hide his assets through an agreement with his mistress. Held: he must support their son Jarren, who is legitimate despite the declaration of nullity of the marriage under Art. 36 (Art. 54). "While one spouse is no longer obligated to legally support the other because their marriage was nullified, his obligation to provide support to his minor child does not cease even if care and custody are no longer with the second spouse."

Which property answers for support (Arts. 197–198)

Art. 197, FC. In case of legitimate ascendants; descendants, whether legitimate or illegitimate; and brothers and sisters, whether legitimately or illegitimately related, only the separate property of the person obliged to give support shall be answerable provided that in case the obligor has no separate property, the absolute community or the conjugal partnership, if financially capable, shall advance the support, which shall be deducted from the share of the spouse obliged upon the liquidation of the absolute community or of the conjugal partnership. (n)
Art. 198, FC. During the proceedings for legal separation or for annulment of marriage, and for declaration of nullity of marriage, the spouses and their children shall be supported from the properties of the absolute community or the conjugal partnership. After the final judgment granting the petition, the obligation of mutual support between the spouses ceases. However, in case of legal separation, the court may order that the guilty spouse shall give support to the innocent one, specifying the terms of such order. (292a)
Tolentino (Art. 198): if there is common property, support pendente lite from it is mandatory. If there is none, may one spouse demand it from the other? In an annulment, yes: the marriage subsists until the decree. In legal separation, a ground committed by the plaintiff would end his or her right. After a final judgment of annulment, the wife may not ask support from the husband even while the liquidation is pending, but the court may order an advance on her share in the conjugal property. In legal separation, support of the innocent spouse by the guilty one is discretionary with the court, not a matter of right.
Lerma v. CA (61 SCRA 440, December 20, 1974)

Teodoro Lerma filed a complaint for adultery against his wife, Concepcion Diaz, and her paramour. She then sued him for legal separation and/or separation of property, custody of the children and support, with an urgent petition for support pendente lite for herself and their youngest son. The trial court granted it. Held: she is not entitled to it. "The right to separate support or maintenance, even from the conjugal partnership property, presupposes the existence of a justifiable cause for the spouse claiming such right to live separately." A petition in bad faith, filed by one who is herself guilty of an act constituting a ground for legal separation, is not within the law granting separate support. Under NCC 303, support ceases when the recipient commits an act giving rise to disinheritance, and under NCC 921 one such cause is "when the spouse has given cause for legal separation." "The loss of the substantive right to support in such a situation is incompatible with any claim for support pendente lite." (The criminal case for adultery against her was then pending in the Court of Appeals; the Court said its ruling did not prejudge it.)

Order of obligors and division (Arts. 199–200)

Art. 199, FC. Whenever two or more persons are obliged to give support, the liability shall devolve upon the following persons in the order herein provided:
(1) The spouse;
(2) The descendants in the nearest degree;
(3) The ascendants in the nearest degree; and
(4) The brothers and sisters. (294a)
Art. 200, FC. When the obligation to give support falls upon two or more persons, the payment of the same shall be divided between them in proportion to the resources of each.
However, in case of urgent need and by special circumstances, the judge may order only one of them to furnish the support provisionally, without prejudice to his right to claim from the other obligors the share due from them.
When two or more recipients at the same time claim support from one and the same person legally obliged to give it, should the latter not have sufficient means to satisfy all claims, the order established in the preceding article shall be followed, unless the concurrent obligees should be the spouse and a child subject to parental authority, in which case the child shall be preferred. (295a)
Uribe (CivRev Quiz 2 continuation): "If the spouse has no capacity to support or may be dead already, the children are required to give support equally, correct?" No. A student answered that it goes automatically to the brothers and sisters, which is also wrong: the next in line are the descendants, and among co-obligors the payment is divided "in proportion to the resources of each" (Art. 200), not equally.
Tolentino (Arts. 199–200): the order follows the closeness of the relationship; remoter relatives are called only in default of nearer ones. The NCC said the descendants and ascendants are liable in the order they would succeed ab intestato; the FC dropped that clause, but Tolentino would still apply it, "as support follows succession": (1) legitimate children and descendants, (2) legitimate parents and ascendants, (3) illegitimate children and their descendants. The action may be brought against any obligor, but the plaintiff must show that those ahead of the defendant have no means; a rich brother is not liable if he proves that the father can give the support. Co-obligors in the same grade are liable jointly, not solidarily, each in proportion to his means; in urgent cases one may be made to give all of it provisionally, with recourse against the others. Among recipients, children under parental authority (legitimate, legitimated, illegitimate or adopted) are preferred over everyone, including the spouse. But that preference applies only when the support comes from the obligor's separate property; if it comes from the ACP or CPG, there is no preference, because it is a charge on the community itself.
Mangonon v. CA (G.R. No. 125041, June 30, 2006; class reviewer)

Ma. Belen Mangonon sought support for her twin daughters, Rica and Rina, then in college in the United States, from their father Federico Delgado and their paternal grandfather Francisco Delgado. The mother herself was financially incapable, relying on loans. Held: Francisco must give the support pendente lite. Federico's claimed inability had no evidence behind it, and the mother could not; "the obligation to give support rests principally on those more closely related to the recipient. However, the more remote relatives may be held to shoulder the responsibility should the claimant prove that those who are called upon to provide support do not have the means to do so." Francisco, owner and director of several corporations, had ample means.

Spouses Lim v. Lim (G.R. No. 163209, October 30, 2009; class reviewer)

A mother sued her husband and his parents for the support of their three children. Held: the paternal grandparents are liable for the deficiency. "The obligation to provide legal support passes on to ascendants not only upon default of the parents but also for the latter's inability to provide sufficient support." But "only descendants by a direct line are entitled to such support": the grandparents had no obligation to support the mother, their daughter-in-law.

Amount, time and manner of payment (Arts. 201–204)

Art. 201, FC. The amount of support, in the cases referred to in Articles 195 and 196, shall be in proportion to the resources or means of the giver and to the necessities of the recipient. (296a)
Art. 202, FC. Support in the cases referred to in the preceding article shall be reduced or increased proportionately, according to the reduction or increase of the necessities of the recipient and the resources or means of the person obliged to furnish the same. (297a)
Art. 203, FC. The obligation to give support shall be demandable from the time the person who has a right to receive the same needs it for maintenance, but it shall not be paid except from the date of judicial or extra-judicial demand.
Support pendente lite may be claimed in accordance with the Rules of Court.
Payment shall be made within the first five days of each corresponding month or when the recipient dies, his heirs shall not be obliged to return what he has received in advance. (298a)
Art. 204, FC. The person obliged to give support shall have the option to fulfill the obligation either by paying the allowance fixed, or by receiving and maintaining in the family dwelling the person who has a right to receive support. The latter alternative cannot be availed of in case there is a moral or legal obstacle thereto. (299a)
Samplex (Uribe quizzes, several versions): "Payment for support shall be made within the first 15 days of each corresponding month" → false: five days (Art. 203). "Payment shall be made within the first five days of each corresponding month" → true. "The person obliged to give support shall fulfill the obligation by paying the allowance fixed" → false: he has the option to pay the allowance or to receive and maintain the recipient in the family dwelling (Art. 204). Its mirror, "shall fulfill the obligation by receiving and maintaining in the family dwelling", is also false; in the class discussion, the point was that "the person obliged to give support has an option", and the second alternative is unavailable when there is a moral or legal obstacle.
Tolentino (Arts. 202–204): a judgment for support is never final; it may always be modified as the obligor's means and the recipient's needs change (Canonizado). A change has no retroactive effect: no refund of what was paid, and arrears are not condoned by a later reduction. A support judgment does not lapse after five years like ordinary judgments; it lasts until the children come of age, though unpaid installments more than ten years old are uncollectible (Florendo v. Organo). It may be enforced by contempt (only for willful disobedience), or by attachment and execution (on mere failure to pay), or both. Art. 204 option has two requisites: the obligor has his own home, and there is no moral or legal obstacle. Obstacles recognized: a husband who abducted the girl and married her to avoid prosecution but never lived with her; a natural father who never supported the child and has since married another woman; a married daughter, who must live with her husband; a wife driven out by the husband's demands for unchaste acts (Goitia v. Campos Rueda), by his tolerance of his son's seduction of her daughter, by repeated infidelity, or by abuse. Emancipated children cannot be compelled to live with the parent to be supported. Where the wife leaves without sufficient cause (e.g., mere jealousy), the husband may insist on supporting her at home, but the court will not order her to live with him. The option may be waived, e.g., by a contract giving the wife custody and promising a pension.
Canonizado v. Benitez (127 SCRA 610, 1984)

A wife had a final judgment for monthly support against her husband. Later he moved to stop paying current support, arguing that she no longer needed it, so that under NCC 303(3) his obligation had ceased. (Facts simplified; your sources give only the doctrine.) Held: NCC 303 "provides that the obligation to give support shall also cease 'when the recipient may engage in a trade, profession, or industry, or has obtained work, or has improved his fortune in such a way that he no longer needs the allowance for his subsistence'. … It does not mean, however, that the obligation to give or the right to ask for support also ceases permanently because the lack of a need for it may only be temporary. In other words, the above circumstances do not affect the right to support between spouses but only the action to make it demandable, such right being born from the law and created as such by the marriage tie." "A judgment for support is never final in the sense that not only can its amount be subject to increase or decrease but its demandability may also be suspended or re-enforced when appropriate circumstances exist." No separate action is needed to suspend current support; the court that rendered the judgment decides it on evidence.

Perkins v. Perkins (57 Phil. 223, 1932; class reviewer)

A wife sued for separate maintenance and claimed reimbursement of expenses long before the suit. Held: "The obligation to provide support only becomes enforceable and payable from the date of the filing of the complaint for separate maintenance. Items of expense that long precede the date of the filing of the suit are generally outside the allowable claims for support." A simple loan of money to the wife is not support.

Exemption, strangers who give support, contractual support (Arts. 205–208)

Art. 205, FC. The right to receive support under this Title as well as any money or property obtained as such support shall not be levied upon on attachment or execution. (302a)
Art. 206, FC. When, without the knowledge of the person obliged to give support, it is given by a stranger, the latter shall have a right to claim the same from the former, unless it appears that he gave it without intention of being reimbursed. (2164a)
Art. 207, FC. When the person obliged to support another unjustly refuses or fails to give support when urgently needed by the latter, any third person may furnish support to the needy individual, with right of reimbursement from the person obliged to give support. This Article shall particularly apply when the father or mother of a child under the age of majority unjustly refuses to support or fails to give support to the child when urgently needed. (2166a)
Art. 208, FC. In case of contractual support or that given by will, the excess in amount beyond that required for legal support shall be subject to levy on attachment or execution.
Furthermore, contractual support shall be subject to adjustment whenever modification is necessary due to changes of circumstances manifestly beyond the contemplation of the parties. (n)
Samplex (Uribe Civil Law Reviewer, Quiz #4, T/F 10): "When, without the knowledge of the person obliged to give support, it is given by a stranger, the latter shall have a right to claim the same from the former." Keyed false: the statement drops the "unless" clause ("unless it appears that he gave it without intention of being reimbursed").
Tolentino (Arts. 205–208): exemption from execution protects the recipient "against want and misery"; but the excess of contractual or testamentary support over legal support may be levied (Art. 208). Requisites under Art. 206: (1) support was furnished to a dependent of one bound to give it who fails to do so, (2) by a stranger, (3) without the obligor's knowledge, (4) not without expectation of recovering it. The obligor must first be given a chance: a sister-in-law who sent money to a wife whose husband had merely reduced his allowance abroad could not recover (Ramirez v. Redfern, 49 Phil. 849). "Stranger" means one with no obligation to support; a relative lower in the Art. 199 order who gives support when a nearer one fails is not a stranger under Art. 206.
Otamias v. Republic (G.R. No. 189516, June 8, 2016; class reviewer)

A retired AFP officer executed a deed assigning 50% of his retirement benefits to his wife and children for their support, then stopped. The RTC ordered automatic deduction; the CA set it aside, citing the exemption of pensions from execution (P.D. 1638) and the AFP's non-party status. Held: the deduction may be enforced. The exemption of pension benefits from execution is a personal statutory right that may be waived, "especially in order to comply with a husband's duty to support" under the Constitution and the Family Code; the deed of assignment was such a waiver.

Support pendente lite and defenses

Rule 61, Sec. 1, Rules of Court. At the commencement of the proper action or proceeding, or at any time prior to the judgment or final order, a verified application for support pendente lite may be filed by any party stating the grounds for the claim and the financial conditions of both parties, and accompanied by affidavits, depositions or other authentic documents in support thereof. (Secs. 2–6: three days to answer; hearing; provisional order considering the applicant's needs, the adverse party's means and the probable outcome; enforcement by execution or contempt.)
Sanchez v. Zulueta (68 Phil. 110, 1939)

A wife sued her husband for support for herself and her child, and asked for support pendente lite. The husband alleged that she had committed adultery and that the child was not his, and asked to present evidence; the lower courts granted support pendente lite without letting him prove it. Held: error. "Adultery on the part of the wife is a valid defense against an action for support (Quintana v. Lerma, 24 Phil. 285). Consequently, as to the child, it is also a defense that it is the fruit of such adulterous relations, for in that case, it would not be the child of the defendant and, hence, would not be entitled to support as such. But as this defense should be established, and not merely alleged, it would be unavailing if proof thereof is not permitted. It is not of course necessary to go fully into the merits of the case, it being sufficient that the court ascertain the kind and amount of evidence which it may deem sufficient to enable it to justly resolve the application, one way or the other, in view of the merely provisional character of the resolution to be entered."

Reyes v. Ines-Luciano (88 SCRA 803, 1979)

Celia Ilustre-Reyes sued her husband Manuel J. C. Reyes for legal separation, alleging that he had twice tried to kill her, and asked for support pendente lite for herself and their three children. He opposed, alleging that she had committed adultery with her physician. The trial court granted ₱5,000 a month, reduced to ₱4,000. Held: affirmed. Adultery is a good defense, but "the alleged adultery must be established by competent evidence"; the husband offered only allegations. The Court also doubted whether adultery would bar support pendente lite here, since the wife sought it from the conjugal property, not from Manuel's personal funds.

Tolentino (pendente lite): if the defendant's answer denies the marriage, thus putting the plaintiff's very status in issue, alimony pendente lite should not be allowed until the marriage is established; nor where the defense is that the plaintiff wife committed adultery. In an action by a child, the defense that he is the fruit of adulterous relations must likewise be established, not merely alleged. The court has no jurisdiction to award support pendente lite to one claiming to be the defendant's son if paternity is denied, and the defendant's lawyer's consent does not confer it. The husband cannot defend by saying he has other persons to support: the wife's claim is preferred, second only to that of children under parental authority. Support pendente lite cannot be ordered in an action for recovery of property against persons with no legal duty of support.

2011 Bar: Is the wife who leaves her husband without just cause entitled to support? (A) No, because the wife must always be submissive and respectful to the husband. (B) Yes, the marriage not having been dissolved, the husband continues to have an obligation to support his wife. (C) No, because in leaving the conjugal home without just cause, she forfeits her right to support. (D) Yes, since the right to receive support is not subject to any condition.

(C). A spouse who leaves without just cause has no right to separate maintenance; at most the husband may exercise his Art. 204 option to support her in the family home. Compare Tolentino: the right to separate maintenance presupposes a cause justifying a separate domicile (Goitia v. Campos Rueda; Lerma).

Renunciation and termination

Tolentino (cessation): under old NCC 303(4), when the recipient is not a compulsory heir (e.g., a brother), the grounds are those of unworthiness found among the causes of disinheritance: an attempt on the life of the obligor or his family; a groundless accusation of a crime punished by six years or more; conviction of adultery or concubinage with the obligor's spouse; and fraud, violence, intimidation or undue influence regarding a will. When support from one obligor ends because of the recipient's act, he may turn to the next in line, unless the cause bars that too: a husband who forced his wife to make a will loses support from her but may ask his children; a daughter who loses support from her parents through prostitution cannot turn to her brothers.
Quiz traps (support): "Support between spouses ceases upon the filing of a petition for legal separation" → false: upon the final judgment (Art. 198); during the case they are supported from the common property. "Future support may be the subject of a compromise" → false (NCC 2035). "The amount of support fixed in a final judgment can no longer be changed" → false (Art. 202; Canonizado). "Co-obligors share equally" → false: in proportion to their resources (Art. 200). "When the spouse and a minor child both claim support from one person who cannot satisfy both, the spouse is preferred" → false: the child under parental authority is preferred (Art. 200). "Arrears in support can never be waived" → false: only future support cannot.

2011 Bar: Spouses X and Y have a minor daughter, Z, who needs support for her education. Both X and Y are financially distressed and cannot give it. Z's other relatives are financially capable. From whom may Z first rightfully demand support? (A) Grandfather. (B) Brother. (C) Uncle. (D) First cousin.

(A) Grandfather. After the parents come the ascendants in the nearest degree, before brothers and sisters (Art. 199); uncles and cousins are not obliged at all (Art. 195). Lim v. Lim: the obligation passes to the grandparents on the parents' inability, not only on their default.

2018 Bar (adapted): Sofia and Semuel, both unmarried, lived together and had three children. Semuel became a naturalized German citizen. Must he support the minor children?

Yes. Parents must support their illegitimate children (Art. 195(4)), with everything indispensable for sustenance, dwelling, clothing, medical attendance, education and transportation (Art. 194). Even if German law did not require it, his refusal to support them while in the Philippines is punishable under R.A. No. 9262 by the territoriality principle (Del Socorro v. Van Wilsem, G.R. No. 193707, December 10, 2014). Compare the 2023 Bar on an American father: his duty to support is governed by his national law, which he must plead and prove, failing which it is presumed to be the same as ours.
